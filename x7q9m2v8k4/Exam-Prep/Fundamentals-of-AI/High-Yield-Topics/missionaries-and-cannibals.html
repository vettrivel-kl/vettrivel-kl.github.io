Missionaries & Cannibals: Concept Mastery & Exam Questions

Course - Fundamentals of Artificial Intelligence (21CSC569J & 21CSC206T)
Topic Classification - State-Space Problem Formulation & Classical AI Puzzles
Exam Priority - Rank 4 (The Dominant 15-Mark Part C & 20-Mark Question)

1. Executive Summary & Problem Definition​

First formalized in artificial intelligence by Saul Amarel in 1968, the Missionaries and Cannibals Problem is a canonical benchmark for testing an agent's ability to formulate real-world constraints into mathematical state-space representations.

The Problem Statement​

  • Initial Setup: Three missionaries (M=3M=3M=3) and three cannibals (C=3C=3C=3) stand on the Left Bank of a river.
  • The Vessel: A small rowboat is moored at the Left Bank.
  • Capacity Constraint: The boat can transport at most two people (111 or 222 passengers) at a time. The boat cannot navigate across the river autonomously; at least one person must be on board to row.
  • The Safety Hazard (Non-Cannibalizing Constraint):
    At no point in time, on either bank of the river, may the number of cannibals strictly exceed the number of missionaries. If cannibals outnumber missionaries on either bank, the missionaries will be eaten.
  • The Objective: Transfer all 6 individuals and the boat safely from the Left Bank to the Right Bank in the minimum number of river crossings.

2. First-Principles Conceptual Deep Dive & Mathematical Modeling​

University evaluators grade state-space problem formulation on whether students provide formal mathematical definitions rather than vague conversational prose.

1. The State-Space Representation: The 3-Tuple Model​

Because the total populations of missionaries and cannibals are strictly conserved across both river banks:

Mleft+Mright=3,Cleft+Cright=3M_{\text{left}} + M_{\text{right}} = 3, \quad C_{\text{left}} + C_{\text{right}} = 3Mleft​+Mright​=3,Cleft​+Cright​=3

the state of the entire system is uniquely and losslessly captured by tracking the population on the Left Bank and the location of the Boat:

s=(M,C,B)s = (M, C, B)s=(M,C,B)

where:

  • M∈{0,1,2,3}M \in \{0, 1, 2, 3\}M∈{0,1,2,3}: The number of missionaries on the Left Bank.
  • C∈{0,1,2,3}C \in \{0, 1, 2, 3\}C∈{0,1,2,3}: The number of cannibals on the Left Bank.
  • B∈{1,0}B \in \{1, 0\}B∈{1,0}: The location of the boat (B=1B = 1B=1 denotes Left Bank; B=0B = 0B=0 denotes Right Bank).

The complementary state of the Right Bank is derived algebraically:

Mright=3−M,Cright=3−C,Bright=1−BM_{\text{right}} = 3 - M, \quad C_{\text{right}} = 3 - C, \quad B_{\text{right}} = 1 - BMright​=3−M,Cright​=3−C,Bright​=1−B

2. Boundary Constraints​

Valid states must obey non-negative integer limits:

0≤M≤3,0≤C≤3,B∈{0,1}0 \le M \le 3, \quad 0 \le C \le 3, \quad B \in \{0, 1\}0≤M≤3,0≤C≤3,B∈{0,1}

3. The Compound Safety Invariant Constraint​

Missionaries are safe on a river bank under two mutually exclusive conditions:

  1. Zero Missionaries: If there are no missionaries present (M=0M = 0M=0), no one can be eaten, regardless of how many cannibals are on that bank.
  2. Missionary Majority or Parity: If missionaries are present (M>0M > 0M>0), their count must be greater than or equal to the count of cannibals (M≥CM \ge CM≥C).

This logic must hold simultaneously for both river banks:

Safe⁡(M,C)  ⟺  [(M==0∨M≥C)  ∧  ((3−M)==0∨(3−M)≥(3−C))]\mathbf{\operatorname{Safe}(M, C) \iff \Big[ (M == 0 \lor M \ge C) \;\land\; ((3 - M) == 0 \lor (3 - M) \ge (3 - C)) \Big]}Safe(M,C)⟺[(M==0∨M≥C)∧((3−M)==0∨(3−M)≥(3−C))]

Rewriting the Invariant for Examination Papers​

  • On the Left Bank: (M==0)∨(M≥C)(M == 0) \lor (M \ge C)(M==0)∨(M≥C)
  • On the Right Bank: (M==3)∨(M≤C)(M == 3) \lor (M \le C)(M==3)∨(M≤C)
  • Dangerous Configurations: Any state where M=1M = 1M=1 and C>1C > 1C>1, or M=2M = 2M=2 and C<2C < 2C<2, is fatal.

4. Initial and Goal State Formalization​

  • Initial State (s0s_0s0​): All 3 missionaries, all 3 cannibals, and the boat are on the Left Bank: s0=(3,3,1)\mathbf{s_0 = (3, 3, 1)}s0​=(3,3,1)
  • Goal State (sGs_GsG​): Zero missionaries, zero cannibals, and no boat remain on the Left Bank: sG=(0,0,0)\mathbf{s_G = (0, 0, 0)}sG​=(0,0,0)

3. Successor Function & Transition Operators​

The 5 Legal River Crossing Operators​

Because the boat capacity is between 1 and 2 passengers (1≤ΔM+ΔC≤21 \le \Delta M + \Delta C \le 21≤ΔM+ΔC≤2), there are exactly 5 legal action operators:

O={(1,0),  (2,0),  (0,1),  (0,2),  (1,1)}\mathcal{O} = \big\{ (1, 0), \; (2, 0), \; (0, 1), \; (0, 2), \; (1, 1) \big\}O={(1,0),(2,0),(0,1),(0,2),(1,1)}

where (ΔM,ΔC)(\Delta M, \Delta C)(ΔM,ΔC) denotes the number of missionaries and cannibals inside the boat during the crossing:

OperatorAction LabelDescription
O1=(1,0)O_1 = (1, 0)O1​=(1,0)Cross1M\text{Cross}_{1M}Cross1M​Transport 1 Missionary across the river

The Transition Function Result⁡(s,a)\operatorname{Result}(s, a)Result(s,a)​

Given current state s=(M,C,B)s = (M, C, B)s=(M,C,B) and operator a=(ΔM,ΔC)a = (\Delta M, \Delta C)a=(ΔM,ΔC):

  1. If Boat is on the Left Bank (B=1B = 1B=1 — Left →\to→ Right Crossing): M′=M−ΔM,C′=C−ΔC,B′=0M' = M - \Delta M, \quad C' = C - \Delta C, \quad B' = 0M′=M−ΔM,C′=C−ΔC,B′=0
  2. If Boat is on the Right Bank (B=0B = 0B=0 — Right →\to→ Left Return): M′=M+ΔM,C′=C+ΔC,B′=1M' = M + \Delta M, \quad C' = C + \Delta C, \quad B' = 1M′=M+ΔM,C′=C+ΔC,B′=1

Preconditions for Transition Validity​

An action aaa is executable in state sss if and only if:

  1. Population Availability: The departing bank must contain at least ΔM\Delta MΔM missionaries and ΔC\Delta CΔC cannibals.
  2. Boundary Adherence: 0≤M′≤30 \le M' \le 30≤M′≤3 and 0≤C′≤30 \le C' \le 30≤C′≤3.
  3. Safety Invariant Satisfaction: Safe⁡(M′,C′)=True\operatorname{Safe}(M', C') = \text{True}Safe(M′,C′)=True.

Every single one of the 16 legal states can be reached from the initial state without violating safety constraints.

The Critical Problem Bottleneck (Steps 5 to 7)​

Most human problem-solvers fail at Step 5 and Step 6. To safely get all missionaries across without leaving any stranded with a cannibal majority:

  • At Step 5, two missionaries cross, leaving exactly (1,1)(1, 1)(1,1) on the Left Bank and creating (2,2)(2, 2)(2,2) on the Right Bank.
  • At Step 6, to avoid stranding missionaries, one missionary AND one cannibal must return together. This counter-intuitive "backtracking" move is required to preserve parity on both sides!
  • At Step 7, the remaining two missionaries cross, bringing all 3 missionaries safely to the Right Bank.

6. Examiner's Grading Rubric & Common Traps​

warning

Top 4 Exam Pitfalls Where Students Lose Marks

  1. Omitting the Boat Variable:
    WRONG: Representing the state as a 2-tuple (M,C)(M, C)(M,C).
    CORRECT: State must be a 3-tuple (M,C,B)(M, C, B)(M,C,B). Without tracking the boat, (3,2)(3, 2)(3,2) with the boat on the Left Bank is fundamentally different from (3,2)(3, 2)(3,2) with the boat on the Right Bank.
  2. Unilateral Safety Check:
    WRONG: Checking only whether M≥CM \ge CM≥C on the Left Bank.
    CORRECT: You must explicitly verify the Right Bank: (3−M==0)∨(3−M≥3−C)(3 - M == 0) \lor (3 - M \ge 3 - C)(3−M==0)∨(3−M≥3−C). If M=2M=2M=2 on the Left Bank, the Right Bank has 1M1M1M and 2C2C2C — the single missionary on the Right Bank is eaten!
  3. Allowing the Ghost Boat:
    WRONG: Permitting an empty boat to cross (ΔM=0,ΔC=0)(\Delta M = 0, \Delta C = 0)(ΔM=0,ΔC=0).
    CORRECT: State explicitly that 1≤ΔM+ΔC≤21 \le \Delta M + \Delta C \le 21≤ΔM+ΔC≤2.
  4. Miscounting Reachable States:
    WRONG: Stating there are 32 reachable states.
    CORRECT: Out of 32 total combinations, only 16 states are legal and reachable.
