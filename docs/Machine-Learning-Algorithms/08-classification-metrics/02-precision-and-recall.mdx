# Precision and Recall

> **Topic —** Overall accuracy treats false positives (false alarms) and false negatives (missed cases) as identical errors. In reality, their business costs are rarely symmetrical. This guide explores **Precision** (selectivity against false alarms) and **Recall** (sensitivity to positive instances), proves why they exist in a fundamental mechanical tradeoff governed by the decision threshold $\tau$, and details Specificity and the Four-Way Metric Symmetry.

---

## 1. The Core Concept

When evaluating a binary classifier, we must evaluate two competing operational perspectives:

1.  **Precision (Positive Predictive Value):** Of all instances the model labeled as positive, what fraction was actually positive?
    $$\text{Precision} = \frac{\text{TP}}{\text{TP} + \text{FP}} = \frac{\text{True Positives}}{\text{Total Predicted Positives}}$$
    *   **Focus:** Minimizing **False Positives (Type I Errors)**. High precision guarantees that when the system raises an alarm, you can trust it.

2.  **Recall (Sensitivity, True Positive Rate):** Of all actual positive cases in the population, what fraction did the model successfully detect?
    $$\text{Recall} = \frac{\text{TP}}{\text{TP} + \text{FN}} = \frac{\text{True Positives}}{\text{Total Actual Positives}}$$
    *   **Focus:** Minimizing **False Negatives (Type II Errors)**. High recall guarantees that few true positive instances slip through undetected.

:::tip

**If you only take one thing from this page**

Precision is about **quality of positive predictions** (few false alarms). Recall is about **quantity of positive cases captured** (few missed cases). Lowering the decision threshold raises Recall but lowers Precision; raising the threshold raises Precision but lowers Recall.

:::

## 3. When to Prioritize Which Metric

The choice between optimizing Precision vs. Recall depends entirely on the **asymmetry of operational costs**:

<Tabs>
  <TabItem value="recall" label="🚨 When Recall is Paramount" default>
    <br/>
    
    *   **The Cost Asymmetry:** False Negatives (missed cases) are dangerous, fatal, or irreversible. False Positives (false alarms) merely incur minor review overhead.
    *   **Domain 1: Medical Cancer Screening:** Missing an early-stage malignant tumor (FN) leads to untreated disease and mortality. A false alarm (FP) is cleared with an ultrasound.
    *   **Domain 2: Airport Security Threat Detection:** Missing a prohibited weapon (FN) compromises flight safety. A false alarm (FP) takes 30 seconds of manual bag inspection.
    *   **Domain 3: Financial Fraud:** Approving a $50,000 fraudulent wire (FN) causes immediate financial loss. A false alarm (FP) sends a one-time confirmation SMS to the cardholder.
    *   **Tuning Rule:** Lower threshold $\tau$ (e.g. $\tau = 0.10 - 0.30$) to cast a wide net.
  </TabItem>
  <TabItem value="precision" label="🎯 When Precision is Paramount">
    <br/>
    
    *   **The Cost Asymmetry:** False Positives (false alarms) destroy user trust, incur heavy legal liability, or trigger expensive irreversible actions. False Negatives are safely tolerated.
    *   **Domain 1: Automated Spam Deletion:** Silently deleting a legitimate CEO contract or college acceptance letter (FP) is catastrophic. Letting an occasional spam email reach the inbox (FN) is a minor nuisance.
    *   **Domain 2: Loan Approval (Auto-Decline):** Falsely denying a highly creditworthy customer (FP) loses high-value long-term interest revenue and damages customer goodwill.
    *   **Domain 3: Recommender Systems (YouTube / E-commerce):** Recommending an irrelevant or offensive video (FP) causes immediate churn. Failing to surface every great video (FN) is unnoticeable to the user.
    *   **Tuning Rule:** Raise threshold $\tau$ (e.g. $\tau = 0.70 - 0.90$) to demand strict statistical certainty before acting.
  </TabItem>
</Tabs>

## 4. The Four-Way Metric Symmetry

Together with Specificity and Negative Predictive Value (NPV), Precision and Recall form a balanced 2x2 mathematical symmetry:

| Perspective | Positive Focus | Negative Focus |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Row Perspective (Actual Classes)** | **Recall (Sensitivity / TPR):**<br/>$$\frac{\text{TP}}{\text{Actual Positives}} = \frac{\text{TP}}{\text{TP} + \text{FN}}$$ | **Specificity (TNR):**<br/>$$\frac{\text{TN}}{\text{Actual Negatives}} = \frac{\text{TN}}{\text{TN} + \text{FP}}$$ |
| **Column Perspective (Predicted Classes)** | **Precision (PPV):**<br/>$$\frac{\text{TP}}{\text{Predicted Positives}} = \frac{\text{TP}}{\text{TP} + \text{FP}}$$ | **Negative Predictive Value (NPV):**<br/>$$\frac{\text{TN}}{\text{Predicted Negatives}} = \frac{\text{TN}}{\text{TN} + \text{FN}}$$ |

### Complementary Error Rates
*   **False Positive Rate (FPR):** $\text{FPR} = 1 - \text{Specificity} = \frac{\text{FP}}{\text{TN} + \text{FP}}$ (Fraction of healthy people falsely alarmed).
*   **False Negative Rate (FNR / Miss Rate):** $\text{FNR} = 1 - \text{Recall} = \frac{\text{FN}}{\text{TP} + \text{FN}}$ (Fraction of sick people overlooked).

## 6. Common Mistakes

<Tabs>
  <TabItem value="m1" label="🚨 1. Confusing Precision and Recall Denominators" default>
    <br/>
    
    *   **The Misconception:** *"Precision and Recall have the same denominator since both start with TP in the numerator."*
    *   **❌ Wrong Thinking:** Dividing TP by total correct or mixing predicted and actual counts.
    *   **✅ The Right Principle:** Precision divides by **Predicted Positives** ($\text{TP} + \text{FP}$, the column total). Recall divides by **Actual Positives** ($\text{TP} + \text{FN}$, the row total).
  </TabItem>
  <TabItem value="m2" label="🚨 2. Believing Both Can Be 100% on Real Data">
    <br/>
    
    *   **The Misconception:** *"With sufficient hyperparameter tuning and deep models, we can reach 100% Precision and 100% Recall."*
    *   **❌ Wrong Thinking:** Expecting perfect separation on overlapping real-world feature distributions.
    *   **✅ The Right Principle:** Unless feature classes are completely linearly separable with zero noise, raising the threshold to eliminate false positives inevitably misses borderline positives. You must make an intentional engineering tradeoff.
  </TabItem>
  <TabItem value="m3" label="🚨 3. Neglecting Baseline Precision">
    <br/>
    
    *   **The Misconception:** *"A model with 60% precision is terrible."*
    *   **❌ Wrong Thinking:** Evaluating precision in a vacuum without knowing class prevalence.
    *   **✅ The Right Principle:** If true fraud prevalence is $0.1\%$ (1 in 1,000), a random guess has a precision of $0.1\%$. Achieving $60\%$ precision represents a **600x diagnostic lift** over baseline! Always contextualize precision against class prevalence.
  </TabItem>
  <TabItem value="m4" label="🚨 4. Confusing Specificity with Precision">
    <br/>
    
    *   **The Misconception:** *"Specificity and Precision both measure how clean our negative classifications are."*
    *   **❌ Wrong Thinking:** Conflating row-wise True Negative Rate with column-wise Positive Predictive Value.
    *   **✅ The Right Principle:** Specificity is $\frac{\text{TN}}{\text{TN} + \text{FP}}$ (row percentage of healthy people correctly cleared). Precision is $\frac{\text{TP}}{\text{TP} + \text{FP}}$ (column percentage of positive calls that were right).
  </TabItem>
  <TabItem value="m5" label="🚨 5. Trivial 100% Recall Trap">
    <br/>
    
    *   **The Misconception:** *"Our model achieved 100% Recall, so our fraud detector is flawless."*
    *   **❌ Wrong Thinking:** Quoting Recall without checking False Positive counts.
    *   **✅ The Right Principle:** Any trivial algorithm that predicts 'Positive' for 100% of inputs automatically achieves 100% Recall ($\text{FN} = 0$). However, its Precision will collapse to baseline prevalence. Always evaluate Precision and Recall in tandem.
  </TabItem>
</Tabs>

**📌 Key Takeaways**

*   **⚖️ Tradeoff is mechanical:** Adjusting the classification threshold $\tau$ moves the operational point along the Precision-Recall curve.
*   **🎯 Align to cost asymmetry:** If false negatives kill or cost millions, tune for Recall; if false alarms destroy customer trust or incur legal liability, tune for Precision.
*   **📊 Never report one in isolation:** A 100% Recall model can be trivial (predict everything positive); always report Precision alongside Recall or combine them via the F1-Score.

### Review Flashcards

<details>
  <summary>❓ <b>1. [THEORY] Why does lowering a classifier's decision threshold mechanically increase Recall while decreasing Precision?</b></summary>
  <div className="answer-content">
    <br/>
    
    *   **More Positives Declared:** Lowering $\tau$ broadens the criteria for predicting positive ($\hat{y} = 1$).
    *   **Effect on Recall:** Borderline true positive cases that previously scored below $\tau$ are now captured as True Positives, reducing $\text{FN} \to 0$ and driving $\text{Recall} = \frac{\text{TP}}{\text{TP} + \text{FN}} \to 100\%$.
    *   **Effect on Precision:** Borderline true negative cases are also swept into positive predictions, increasing False Positives ($\text{FP} \uparrow$) and diluting $\text{Precision} = \frac{\text{TP}}{\text{TP} + \text{FP}}$.
  </div>
</details>

<details>
  <summary>❓ <b>2. [MATH] A model evaluates 200 samples: TP = 70, FP = 10, FN = 30, TN = 90. Calculate Precision, Recall, and Specificity.</b></summary>
  <div className="answer-content">
    <br/>
    
    *   **Precision:** $\frac{\text{TP}}{\text{TP} + \text{FP}} = \frac{70}{70 + 10} = \frac{70}{80} = 0.875$ ($87.5\%$).
    *   **Recall:** $\frac{\text{TP}}{\text{TP} + \text{FN}} = \frac{70}{70 + 30} = \frac{70}{100} = 0.700$ ($70.0\%$).
    *   **Specificity:** $\frac{\text{TN}}{\text{TN} + \text{FP}} = \frac{90}{90 + 10} = \frac{90}{100} = 0.900$ ($90.0\%$).
  </div>
</details>

  <div className="answer-content">
    <br/>
    
    *   **Cost Asymmetry:** Banning an innocent user permanently (False Positive) creates intense customer outrage, PR backlash, and potential support tickets.
    *   Letting a mildly toxic comment slip through (False Negative) is an acceptable compromise that can be addressed via manual community flagging later.
    *   Automated account actions require extreme precision ($99\%+$) before applying automated punitive actions.
  </div>
</details>

<details>
  <summary>❓ <b>4. [PRACTICE] In an imbalanced fraud dataset (1% fraud), what is the precision of a naive random guessing baseline?</b></summary>
  <div className="answer-content">
    <br/>
    
    *   **Prevalence Rate:** The baseline precision of a random or constant positive guesser is equal to the **positive class prevalence**:
        $$\text{Baseline Precision} = \frac{\text{Actual Positives}}{\text{Total Samples}} = \frac{1}{100} = 1.0\%$$
    *   If a complex neural net achieves $30\%$ precision on this dataset, it represents a massive **30x diagnostic improvement** over random guessing, even though $30\%$ sounds low in the abstract.
  </div>
</details>

<details>
  <summary>❓ <b>5. [THEORY] What is the difference between Specificity and Negative Predictive Value (NPV)?</b></summary>
  <div className="answer-content">
    <br/>
    
    *   **Specificity (TNR):** Row perspective. $\frac{\text{TN}}{\text{TN} + \text{FP}}$. Measures how effectively the model clears actual healthy people. Independent of class prevalence.
    *   **Negative Predictive Value (NPV):** Column perspective. $\frac{\text{TN}}{\text{TN} + \text{FN}}$. Answers: When the model predicts 'Negative', what is the probability the patient is truly healthy? Heavily dependent on class prevalence.
  </div>
</details>
