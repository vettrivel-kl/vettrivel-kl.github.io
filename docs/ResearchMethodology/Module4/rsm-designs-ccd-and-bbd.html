RSM Designs: Central Composite & Box-Behnken

Topic - When a process nears an optimum, first-order planar models exhibit significant lack-of-fit due to physical curvature. To fit a full second-order polynomial response surface with quadratic and interaction terms, experimenters transition to two specialized experimental designs: the Central Composite Design (CCD) and the Box-Behnken Design (BBD).

1. Second-Order Modeling & The Need for Curvature Designs​

A standard two-level factorial (2k2^k2k) design only samples the boundary corners of the design space. It provides information on main effects and linear interactions, but cannot estimate pure quadratic curvature (βiiXi2\beta_{ii} X_i^2βii​Xi2​).

To capture curvature, each factor must be evaluated at a minimum of three distinct levels. Response Surface Methodology addresses this requirement through quadratic polynomial models:

Y=β0+∑i=1kβiXi+∑i=1kβiiXi2+∑i<jβijXiXj+ϵY = \beta_0 + \sum_{i=1}^k \beta_i X_i + \sum_{i=1}^k \beta_{ii} X_i^2 + \sum_{i \lt j} \beta_{ij} X_i X_j + \epsilonY=β0​+i=1∑k​βi​Xi​+i=1∑k​βii​Xi2​+i<j∑​βij​Xi​Xj​+ϵ

For a two-factor system (k=2k = 2k=2):

Y=β0+β1X1+β2X2+β12X1X2+β11X12+β22X22+ϵY = \beta_0 + \beta_1 X_1 + \beta_2 X_2 + \beta_{12} X_1 X_2 + \beta_{11} X_1^2 + \beta_{22} X_2^2 + \epsilonY=β0​+β1​X1​+β2​X2​+β12​X1​X2​+β11​X12​+β22​X22​+ϵ

Where:

  • β0\beta_0β0​ = Intercept (predicted baseline at center conditions).
  • β1,β2\beta_1, \beta_2β1​,β2​ = Linear main effects.
  • β12\beta_{12}β12​ = Cross-product interaction effect.
  • β11,β22\beta_{11}, \beta_{22}β11​,β22​ = Quadratic curvature effects.

2. Central Composite Design (CCD)​

Core Definition & Purpose​

The Central Composite Design (CCD) is the most widely utilized experimental design in Response Surface Methodology. It is specifically structured to evaluate:

  • Linear effects
  • Interaction effects
  • Quadratic (curvature) effects
  • Optimum operating setpoints

A CCD is created by augmenting an existing 2k2^k2k factorial design with axial (star) points and replicated center points.

The Three Components of CCD Points​

A CCD is partitioned into three distinct geometric categories:

A. Factorial Points (2k2^k2k)​

Vertices of the kkk-dimensional hypercube evaluated at coded coordinates (±1,±1,… )(\pm 1, \pm 1, \dots)(±1,±1,…).

  • For two factors (k=2k = 2k=2): 22=42^2 = 422=4 corner runs:
    • Run 1: (−1,−1)(-1, -1)(−1,−1)
    • Run 2: (−1,+1)(-1, +1)(−1,+1)
    • Run 3: (+1,−1)(+1, -1)(+1,−1)
    • Run 4: (+1,+1)(+1, +1)(+1,+1)
  • Estimation Role: Main effects and interaction effects.

B. Axial / Star Points (2k2k2k)​

Points placed symmetrically along each factor axis at distance ±α\pm \alpha±α from the center, with all other factor coordinates set to zero:

  • For two factors (k=2k = 2k=2): 2(2)=42(2) = 42(2)=4 axial runs:
    • Run 5: (−α,0)(-\alpha, 0)(−α,0)
    • Run 6: (+α,0)(+\alpha, 0)(+α,0)
    • Run 7: (0,−α)(0, -\alpha)(0,−α)
    • Run 8: (0,+α)(0, +\alpha)(0,+α)
  • Estimation Role: Pure quadratic curvature effects (β11,β22\beta_{11}, \beta_{22}β11​,β22​).

C. Center Points (ncn_cnc​)​

Replicated trials conducted at the origin (0,0,…,0)(0, 0, \dots, 0)(0,0,…,0).

  • For two factors with nc=5n_c = 5nc​=5: Runs 9, 10, 11, 12, 13 at (0,0)(0, 0)(0,0).
  • Estimation Role: Estimate pure experimental error variance (σ2\sigma^2σ2), detect global curvature, and assess model adequacy.

Derivation of Total Number of Experiments​

For kkk factors, the total number of experimental runs in a standard CCD is given by:

N=2k+2k+nc\boxed{N = 2^k + 2k + n_c}N=2k+2k+nc​​

Where:

  • 2k2^k2k = Number of factorial cube points.
  • 2k2k2k = Number of axial (star) points.
  • ncn_cnc​ = Number of center point replications.

Example Calculation (k=2k = 2k=2 factors, nc=5n_c = 5nc​=5 center points):

N=22+2(2)+5N=4+4+5N=13 experimental runs\begin{aligned} N &= 2^2 + 2(2) + 5 \\ N &= 4 + 4 + 5 \\ \mathbf{N} &= \mathbf{13 \text{ experimental runs}} \end{aligned}NNN​=22+2(2)+5=4+4+5=13 experimental runs​

The Meaning and Derivation of α\alphaα (Star Distance)​

The parameter α\alphaα designates the radial distance of the axial points from the design center.

To establish rotatability (meaning the prediction variance Var[y^(x)]\text{Var}[\hat{y}(\mathbf{x})]Var[y^​(x)] depends solely on the distance from the design center and is invariant to direction), α\alphaα is derived as:

α=(2k)14\boxed{\alpha = (2^k)^{\frac{1}{4}}}α=(2k)41​​

For k=2k = 2k=2 Factors:

α=(22)14=414=2≈1.414\alpha = (2^2)^{\frac{1}{4}} = 4^{\frac{1}{4}} = \sqrt{2} \approx \mathbf{1.414}α=(22)41​=441​=2​≈1.414

Thus, the axial coordinates for a rotatable two-factor CCD are:

(±1.414,0)and(0,±1.414)(\pm 1.414, 0) \quad \text{and} \quad (0, \pm 1.414)(±1.414,0)and(0,±1.414)

Engineering Interpretation of Axial Coordinates

The calculated values 91.72∘C,148.28∘C,7.93 psi,91.72^\circ\text{C}, 148.28^\circ\text{C}, 7.93\,\text{psi},91.72∘C,148.28∘C,7.93psi, and 22.07 psi22.07\,\text{psi}22.07psi are not final optimum values. The 11 Steps in CCD Execution​

  1. Define the problem and response: Formulate the objective (e.g., maximize yield).
  2. Select important factors: Identify the continuous inputs (X1,X2X_1, X_2X1​,X2​).
  3. Choose factor ranges: Determine realistic upper and lower operational limits.
  4. Code the factor values: Normalize physical scales to (−1,0,+1)(-1, 0, +1)(−1,0,+1).
  5. Construct the CCD matrix: Assemble Factorial points (2k2^k2k) + Axial points (2k2k2k) + Center points (ncn_cnc​).
  6. Conduct experiments and record responses: Execute trials in randomized order.
  7. Fit the second-order model: Estimate coefficients (β0,βi,βii,βij\beta_0, \beta_i, \beta_{ii}, \beta_{ij}β0​,βi​,βii​,βij​).
  8. Perform ANOVA diagnostics: Evaluate FFF-value, ppp-value, R2R^2R2, Adjusted R2R^2R2, and Lack-of-Fit.
  9. Generate response surfaces: Produce 2D contour maps and 3D topographical surface plots.
  10. Determine optimum factor settings: Calculate the stationary point coordinates (x0\mathbf{x}_0x0​).
  11. Conduct a confirmation experiment: Physically test the predicted optimum setting.

3. Box-Behnken Design (BBD)​

Core Definition & Purpose​

The Box-Behnken Design (BBD) is an independent, spherical three-level design (−1,0,+1-1, 0, +1−1,0,+1) used in Response Surface Methodology to fit second-order models without requiring axial star points.

It is particularly advantageous when:

  • There are 333 or more factors (k≥3k \ge 3k≥3).
  • Factors are evaluated at three discrete settings: Low (−1-1−1), Middle (000), and High (+1+1+1).
  • Experiments at extreme corner combinations are undesirable or hazardous (e.g., combining high temperature, high pressure, and high catalyst concentration might cause an explosion or equipment burnout).

Geometric Architecture: Edge Midpoints​

Unlike CCD, Box-Behnken does not use the corner points of the cubic domain (e.g., (+1,+1,+1)(+1, +1, +1)(+1,+1,+1) is excluded) and contains no star points outside the cube (α\alphaα points).

Instead, experimental points are located exclusively at the midpoints of the edges of the design space and at the center:

Derivation of Number of Experiments in BBD​

For kkk factors, the total number of experimental trials in a standard Box-Behnken Design is:

N=2k(k−1)+nc\boxed{N = 2k(k - 1) + n_c}N=2k(k−1)+nc​​

Where:

  • kkk = Number of factors (k≥3k \ge 3k≥3).
  • 2k(k−1)2k(k - 1)2k(k−1) = Number of edge midpoint points.
  • ncn_cnc​ = Number of center points.

Example Calculation (k=3k = 3k=3 factors, nc=3n_c = 3nc​=3 center points):

N=2(3)(3−1)+3N=2(3)(2)+3N=12+3N=15 experimental runs\begin{aligned} N &= 2(3)(3 - 1) + 3 \\ N &= 2(3)(2) + 3 \\ N &= 12 + 3 \\ \mathbf{N} &= \mathbf{15 \text{ experimental runs}} \end{aligned}NNNN​=2(3)(3−1)+3=2(3)(2)+3=12+3=15 experimental runs​

(By comparison, a 3-factor CCD requires 23+2(3)+3=172^3 + 2(3) + 3 = 1723+2(3)+3=17 runs minimum, and up to 20 runs with recommended center replicates).

Factor Coding in BBD​

The standard coding transformation equation is:

Xi=xi−xi,cΔxiX_i = \frac{x_i - x_{i, c}}{\Delta x_i}Xi​=Δxi​xi​−xi,c​​

Where xix_ixi​ is actual value, xi,cx_{i, c}xi,c​ is center value, and Δxi\Delta x_iΔxi​ is half-range.

For Temperature (100∘C100^\circ\text{C}100∘C to 140∘C140^\circ\text{C}140∘C):

X1=T−12020  ⟹  {X1=−1→100∘CX1=0→120∘CX1=+1→140∘CX_1 = \frac{T - 120}{20} \implies \begin{cases} X_1 = -1 \to 100^\circ\text{C} \\ X_1 = 0 \to 120^\circ\text{C} \\ X_1 = +1 \to 140^\circ\text{C} \end{cases}X1​=20T−120​⟹⎩⎨⎧​X1​=−1→100∘CX1​=0→120∘CX1​=+1→140∘C​

Critical Property: Unlike CCD (which pushes star points outside to ±1.414\pm 1.414±1.414), BBD keeps all experimental runs strictly within the specified factor range.

The 12 Steps in BBD Execution​

  1. Define the problem: Clarify the process objective.
  2. Select the response: Specify output YYY (e.g., Y=YieldY = \text{Yield}Y=Yield).
  3. Identify important factors: Select continuous parameters (e.g., Temperature, Pressure, Time).
  4. Select three levels: Establish (−1,0,+1)(-1, 0, +1)(−1,0,+1) values.
  5. Construct the BBD matrix: Assemble 2k(k−1)2k(k-1)2k(k−1) edge points + ncn_cnc​ center points.
  6. Conduct the experiments: Run trials in randomized sequence.
  7. Record the responses: Collect quantitative performance metrics.
  8. Fit the second-order model: Estimate full quadratic coefficients.
  9. Perform ANOVA: Verify FFF-value, ppp-value, R2R^2R2, Adjusted R2R^2R2, and Lack-of-Fit.
  10. Generate response surface & contour plots: Inspect 2D/3D interaction topologies.
  11. Determine optimum factor settings: Solve for optimal coordinate vector.
  12. Conduct a confirmation experiment: Verify mathematical predictions empirically.

5. Exam Traps & Operational Nuances​

  • 1. The 2-Factor BBD Fallacy
  • 2. The Safety Factor in Chemical Synthesis

  • The Trap: Attempting to build a Box-Behnken design for k=2k = 2k=2 factors.
  • The Reality: Box-Behnken designs do not exist for k=2k = 2k=2. By definition, an edge midpoint requires at least 3 dimensions to leave one factor at center while varying the other two (2k(k−1)=2(2)(1)=42k(k-1) = 2(2)(1) = 42k(k−1)=2(2)(1)=4, which degenerates into an unrotated square). For k=2k=2k=2, always use a Central Composite Design (CCD).

  • The Trap: Choosing a CCD when factor extremes can cause thermal runaway or mechanical breakdown.
  • The Reality: In a rotatable CCD, axial points push the factors to ±1.414\pm 1.414±1.414 times the nominal range, and corner points test all high extremes simultaneously. If exceeding 140∘C140^\circ\text{C}140∘C or combining high pressure with high temperature is dangerous, BBD is the mandatory industry choice.

CCD Run Formulas

  • Total Runs: N=2k+2k+ncN = 2^k + 2k + n_cN=2k+2k+nc​.

  • Points: Cube (2k2^k2k) + Star (2k2k2k) + Center (ncn_cnc​).

BBD Run Formulas

  • Total Runs: N=2k(k−1)+ncN = 2k(k - 1) + n_cN=2k(k−1)+nc​ (k≥3k \ge 3k≥3).
  • Levels: Strictly 3 levels (−1,0,+1-1, 0, +1−1,0,+1).
  • Points: Edge midpoints only; zero corners, zero stars.

Key Takeaways

  • Curvature Necessity: Estimating quadratic curvature parameters (βii\beta_{ii}βii​) strictly requires testing at three or more factor levels.
  • CCD Rotatability: Central Composite Designs provide rotatable variance contours by extending axial star points to distance α=(2k)1/4\alpha = (2^k)^{1/4}α=(2k)1/4.
  • BBD Operational Safety: Box-Behnken designs protect fragile or hazardous experimental units by omitting extreme factorial corners and eliminating out-of-range star points.
