Physics & Swarm Algorithms: SA & PSO

Topic - When optimizing high-dimensional, non-differentiable engineering systems, physics-based and swarm-based algorithms provide powerful alternatives to evolutionary methods. Simulated Annealing (SA) exploits thermodynamic annealing principles to escape local minima via probabilistic uphill transitions, while Particle Swarm Optimization (PSO) coordinates decentralized swarm intelligence through inertia, personal memory, and social collaboration.

2. Simulated Annealing (SA)​

Physical Metallurgy Analogy​

Simulated Annealing simulates the thermodynamic process of heating a metal alloy above its melting point and cooling it gradually:

  1. Heating Stage: High thermal energy enhances particle mobility, freeing atoms from existing crystalline dislocations.
  2. Isothermal Phase: Particles collide and exchange thermal energy at constant temperature within an equilibrium boundary.
  3. Cooling Stage: Gradual thermal decay reduces atomic kinetic motion, freezing molecules into the lowest possible energy crystal lattice.

The Metropolis Acceptance Criterion​

In a cost minimization problem, let ΔE=f(xnew)−f(xcurrent)\Delta E = f(\mathbf{x}_{\text{new}}) - f(\mathbf{x}_{\text{current}})ΔE=f(xnew​)−f(xcurrent​).

  • If ΔE≤0\Delta E \le 0ΔE≤0 (downhill move toward lower cost): Always Accept (xcurrent←xnew\mathbf{x}_{\text{current}} \leftarrow \mathbf{x}_{\text{new}}xcurrent​←xnew​).
  • If ΔE>0\Delta E \gt 0ΔE>0 (uphill move toward higher cost): Accept with Metropolis Probability (PPP):
P=exp⁡(−ΔET)\boxed{P = \exp\left(-\frac{\Delta E}{T}\right)}P=exp(−TΔE​)​

A uniform random number r∼U(0,1)r \sim \mathcal{U}(0, 1)r∼U(0,1) is drawn. If r<Pr \lt Pr<P, the inferior solution is accepted.

Temperature Trajectory & Cooling Schedules​

  • High Temperature (T→∞T \to \inftyT→∞): P≈1P \approx 1P≈1. The algorithm accepts virtually every uphill move, exploring the entire landscape and jumping freely out of deep local valleys.
  • Low Temperature (T→0T \to 0T→0): P→0P \to 0P→0. Uphill moves are rejected; the algorithm behaves as a greedy local gradient descent, refining the solution.

3. Particle Swarm Optimization (PSO)​

Biological Metaphor & Swarm Mechanics​

Introduced by James Kennedy and Russell Eberhart (1995), PSO simulates the collective foraging intelligence of bird flocks. A swarm of MMM particles navigates a continuous DDD-dimensional search space without centralized coordination.

Each particle iii tracks three coordinates:

  1. Current Position (Xit\mathbf{X}_i^tXit​): Coordinates in the problem domain.
  2. Personal Best (pbesti\mathbf{pbest}_ipbesti​): The highest-fitness position achieved by particle iii across all iterations.
  3. Global Best (gbest\mathbf{gbest}gbest): The highest-fitness coordinate discovered across the entire swarm.

4. Exam Traps & Operational Nuances​

  • 1. Cooling Schedule Too Fast (Quenching)
  • 2. PSO Velocity Explosion

  • The Trap: Reducing temperature too rapidly (e.g., α=0.5\alpha = 0.5α=0.5).
  • The Consequence: Known in metallurgy as quenching. Thermal motion vanishes before molecules escape crystalline dislocations, causing the algorithm to freeze permanently in a local minimum.
  • The Correction: Maintain gradual cooling (0.85≤α≤0.980.85 \le \alpha \le 0.980.85≤α≤0.98).

  • The Trap: Leaving acceleration parameters unbounded without velocity clamping (VmaxV_{\text{max}}Vmax​).
  • The Consequence: When particles are far from gbest\mathbf{gbest}gbest, velocity terms compound exponentially, throwing particles outside the problem boundaries.
  • The Correction: Always enforce velocity clamping: −Vmax≤Vi≤Vmax-V_{\text{max}} \le V_i \le V_{\text{max}}−Vmax​≤Vi​≤Vmax​.

Key Takeaways

  • Asymptotic Optimality: Simulated Annealing guarantees asymptotic convergence to the global optimum provided the temperature decreases logarithmically or sufficiently slowly.
  • Swarm Consensus: Particle Swarm Optimization excels in continuous multi-dimensional search spaces by balancing individual memory (pbest\mathbf{pbest}pbest) against swarm consensus (gbest\mathbf{gbest}gbest).
  • Hyperparameter Discipline: Algorithm performance depends strictly on cooling parameters in SA and velocity clamping/inertia tuning in PSO.
