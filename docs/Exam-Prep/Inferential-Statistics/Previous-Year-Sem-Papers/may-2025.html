(ii) Coefficients of Skewness and Kurtosis in Data Analysis (8 Marks)​

1. Coefficient of Skewness​

Skewness measures the degree of asymmetry of a distribution around its mean.

  • Karl Pearson's Formula: Skp=Mean−Modeσor3(Mean−Median)σSk_p = \frac{\text{Mean} - \text{Mode}}{\sigma} \quad \text{or} \quad \frac{3(\text{Mean} - \text{Median})}{\sigma}Skp​=σMean−Mode​orσ3(Mean−Median)​
  • Moment-Based Measure (β1,γ1\beta_1, \gamma_1β1​,γ1​): γ1=β1=μ3μ23/2=μ3σ3\gamma_1 = \sqrt{\beta_1} = \frac{\mu_3}{\mu_2^{3/2}} = \frac{\mu_3}{\sigma^3}γ1​=β1​​=μ23/2​μ3​​=σ3μ3​​
  • Interpretation:
    • γ1=0\gamma_1 = 0γ1​=0: Symmetric distribution (Normal distribution).
    • γ1>0\gamma_1 > 0γ1​>0: Positively skewed (tail extends to the right; Mean>Median>Mode\text{Mean} > \text{Median} > \text{Mode}Mean>Median>Mode).
    • γ1<0\gamma_1 < 0γ1​<0: Negatively skewed (tail extends to the left; Mean<Median<Mode\text{Mean} < \text{Median} < \text{Mode}Mean<Median<Mode).
2. Coefficient of Kurtosis​

Kurtosis measures the peakedness and tail-weight (outlier proneness) of a frequency distribution relative to a normal curve.

  • Moment-Based Measure (β2,γ2\beta_2, \gamma_2β2​,γ2​): β2=μ4μ22=μ4σ4,γ2=β2−3\beta_2 = \frac{\mu_4}{\mu_2^2} = \frac{\mu_4}{\sigma^4}, \quad \gamma_2 = \beta_2 - 3β2​=μ22​μ4​​=σ4μ4​​,γ2​=β2​−3
  • Interpretation:
    • β2=3\beta_2 = 3β2​=3 (γ2=0\gamma_2 = 0γ2​=0): Mesokurtic (standard Gaussian bell shape).
    • β2>3\beta_2 > 3β2​>3 (γ2>0\gamma_2 > 0γ2​>0): Leptokurtic (sharp central peak with heavy, fat tails; indicates high outlier probability).
    • β2<3\beta_2 < 3β2​<3 (γ2<0\gamma_2 < 0γ2​<0): Platykurtic (flat-topped curve with thin, light tails).

Solution 3 (20 Marks)​

1. The Binomial Distribution (6 Marks)​

Definition & Mathematical Model:​

Models the number of successes kkk in nnn independent and identical Bernoulli trials with constant probability of success ppp: P(X=k)=(nk)pk(1−p)n−k,k∈{0,1,…,n}P(X = k) = \binom{n}{k} p^k (1 - p)^{n - k}, \quad k \in \{0, 1, \dots, n\}P(X=k)=(kn​)pk(1−p)n−k,k∈{0,1,…,n} Mean: μ=np,Variance: σ2=npq(Variance < Mean)\text{Mean: } \mu = np, \quad \text{Variance: } \sigma^2 = npq \quad (\text{Variance } < \text{ Mean})Mean: μ=np,Variance: σ2=npq(Variance < Mean)

Role in Data Analysis:​
  • Binary Outcome Modeling: Serves as the foundation for A/B testing conversion rates, customer churn, and click-through analysis.
  • Acceptance Sampling in Manufacturing: Evaluates lot quality by determining the probability of observing kkk defective units in a sample of size nnn.
  • Concrete Example: In clinical trials testing a vaccine with an 80%80\%80% efficacy rate (p=0.80p = 0.80p=0.80) on n=20n = 20n=20 patients, calculating the probability that at least 18 patients develop immunity.

2. The Poisson Distribution (7 Marks)​

Definition & Mathematical Model:​

Models the frequency of rare, independent events occurring over a continuous interval of time, area, or volume at a constant average rate λ\lambdaλ: P(X=k)=e−λλkk!,k∈{0,1,2,… }P(X = k) = \frac{e^{-\lambda} \lambda^k}{k!}, \quad k \in \{0, 1, 2, \dots\}P(X=k)=k!e−λλk​,k∈{0,1,2,…} Mean: E[X]=λ,Variance: Var⁡(X)=λ(Equidispersion)\text{Mean: } \mathbb{E}[X] = \lambda, \quad \text{Variance: } \operatorname{Var}(X) = \lambda \quad (\text{Equidispersion})Mean: E[X]=λ,Variance: Var(X)=λ(Equidispersion)

Role in Data Analysis:​
  • Arrival Processes & Queueing Theory: Models customer traffic at bank tellers, website server hits per second, and network packet arrivals.
  • Rare Event Reliability Modeling: Accurately models industrial equipment breakdowns, insurance claims per year, or defects per square meter of fabric.
  • Concrete Example: If a cloud web server experiences an average of λ=4\lambda = 4λ=4 downtime incidents per month, calculating the probability of zero incidents in a given month (P(X=0)=e−4≈0.0183P(X=0) = e^{-4} \approx 0.0183P(X=0)=e−4≈0.0183).

Solution 4 (20 Marks)​

(i) Power of a Statistical Test (10 Marks)​

1. Mathematical Definition​

The Power of a Hypothesis Test is the probability of correctly rejecting a false null hypothesis:

Power=1−β=P(Reject H0∣H1 is true)\text{Power} = 1 - \beta = P(\text{Reject } H_0 \mid H_1 \text{ is true})Power=1−β=P(Reject H0​∣H1​ is true)

where β\betaβ is the probability of committing a Type II Error (failing to detect a real effect).

2. Decision Matrix​
Reality \ DecisionFail to Reject H0H_0H0​Reject H0H_0H0​

3. Determinants of Test Power​
  1. Effect Size (δ=μ1−μ0\delta = \mu_1 - \mu_0δ=μ1​−μ0​): Larger true differences between groups are easier to detect, yielding higher power.
  2. Sample Size (nnn): Increasing nnn shrinks standard error (σ/n\sigma / \sqrt{n}σ/n​), tightening the sampling distribution and increasing power.
  3. Significance Level (α\alphaα): Relaxing α\alphaα from 0.010.010.01 to 0.050.050.05 enlarges the rejection region, lowering β\betaβ and raising power.
  4. Population Variance (σ2\sigma^2σ2): Lower noise/variability increases test power.
4. Applied Example​

In pharmaceutical drug trials, a power of 0.800.800.80 (80%80\%80%) means that if a new drug is genuinely effective, the trial has an 80%80\%80% probability of achieving a statistically significant result, with a 20%20\%20% risk of missing the medical breakthrough (β=0.20\beta = 0.20β=0.20).
