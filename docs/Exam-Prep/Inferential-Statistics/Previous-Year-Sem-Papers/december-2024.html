(i) Measures of Dispersion and Grouped Data Calculation (10 Marks)​

1. Concept and Role of Measures of Dispersion​

Measures of central tendency (mean, median) identify the central location of a distribution, but they reveal nothing about the variability among observations.

  • Definition: Measures of dispersion quantify the extent to which individual observations deviate from the central value or scatter across the domain.
  • Significance:
    1. Assesses the reliability of the central average (low dispersion indicates a highly representative mean).
    2. Enables meaningful comparisons of consistency and risk across different distributions via relative measures like the Coefficient of Variation (CV).

If each test is conducted at significance level α=0.05\alpha = 0.05α=0.05, the Family-Wise Type I Error Rate (αFW\alpha_{\text{FW}}αFW​) explodes: αFW=1−(1−α)C=1−(0.95)6=1−0.7351=0.2649 (26.49%)\alpha_{\text{FW}} = 1 - (1 - \alpha)^C = 1 - (0.95)^6 = 1 - 0.7351 = \mathbf{0.2649 \ (26.49\%)}αFW​=1−(1−α)C=1−(0.95)6=1−0.7351=0.2649 (26.49%)

  • The Hazard: There is a 26.5%26.5\%26.5% chance of finding at least one falsely significant difference purely by random chance.
  • ANOVA Solution: ANOVA tests all kkk groups simultaneously in a single omnibus test while strictly preserving the Type I error rate at α=0.05\alpha = 0.05α=0.05.
