Question 1​

info

Question Statement

A beverage company claims that the average sugar content in their new energy drink is 15 grams per can with a standard deviation of 3 grams. A random sample of 50 cans is tested, and the sample mean sugar content is found to be 14 grams. Is there sufficient evidence at the 0.05 significance level to conclude that the mean sugar content is different from 15 grams? Use the critical region technique, p-value technique, and confidence interval technique to test the claim.

Solution 1​

Hypotheses & Given Parameters​
  • Null Hypothesis (H0H_0H0​): μ=15 g\mu = 15\text{ g}μ=15 g
  • Alternative Hypothesis (HaH_aHa​): μ≠15 g\mu \neq 15\text{ g}μ=15 g (Two-tailed test, α=0.05  ⟹  α/2=0.025\alpha = 0.05 \implies \alpha/2 = 0.025α=0.05⟹α/2=0.025)
  • Parameters: μ0=15 g, xˉ=14 g, σ=3 g, n=50 cans\mu_0 = 15\text{ g}, \, \bar{x} = 14\text{ g}, \, \sigma = 3\text{ g}, \, n = 50\text{ cans}μ0​=15 g,xˉ=14 g,σ=3 g,n=50 cans (n≥30  ⟹  Zn \ge 30 \implies Zn≥30⟹Z-test)
Standard Error & Test Statistic​

SE=σn=350≈0.4243 gSE = \frac{\sigma}{\sqrt{n}} = \frac{3}{\sqrt{50}} \approx \mathbf{0.4243\text{ g}}SE=n​σ​=50​3​≈0.4243 g Zcal=xˉ−μ0SE=14−150.4243≈−2.36Z_{\text{cal}} = \frac{\bar{x} - \mu_0}{SE} = \frac{14 - 15}{0.4243} \approx \mathbf{-2.36}Zcal​=SExˉ−μ0​​=0.424314−15​≈−2.36

Method 1: Critical Region Technique​
  • Critical Values: Zcrit=±1.96Z_{\text{crit}} = \mathbf{\pm 1.96}Zcrit​=±1.96
  • Rejection Rule: Reject H0H_0H0​ if Zcal<−1.96Z_{\text{cal}} < -1.96Zcal​<−1.96 or Zcal>+1.96Z_{\text{cal}} > +1.96Zcal​>+1.96.
  • Decision: Zcal=−2.36<−1.96  ⟹  Z_{\text{cal}} = -2.36 < -1.96 \impliesZcal​=−2.36<−1.96⟹ Reject H0H_0H0​. Mean sugar content is significantly different from 15g (α=0.05\alpha = 0.05α=0.05).
Method 2: P-Value Technique​
  • Calculation: p-value=2×P(Z<−2.36)=2×0.0091=0.0182p\text{-value} = 2 \times P(Z < -2.36) = 2 \times 0.0091 = \mathbf{0.0182}p-value=2×P(Z<−2.36)=2×0.0091=0.0182
  • Decision: p-value=0.0182≤0.05  ⟹  p\text{-value} = 0.0182 \le 0.05 \impliesp-value=0.0182≤0.05⟹ Reject H0H_0H0​. The difference is statistically significant.
Method 3: Confidence Interval Technique​
  • 95% CI: xˉ±(1.96×SE)=14±(1.96×0.4243)=14±0.8316=[13.17 g, 14.83 g]\bar{x} \pm (1.96 \times SE) = 14 \pm (1.96 \times 0.4243) = 14 \pm 0.8316 = \mathbf{[13.17\text{ g}, \, 14.83\text{ g}]}xˉ±(1.96×SE)=14±(1.96×0.4243)=14±0.8316=[13.17 g,14.83 g]
  • Decision: The claimed mean of 15 g15\text{ g}15 g lies outside the interval (15∉[13.17,14.83]15 \notin [13.17, 14.83]15∈/[13.17,14.83])   ⟹  \implies⟹ Reject H0H_0H0​.

Question 2​

info

Question Statement

An electronics manufacturer claims that the average battery life of their latest smartphone model is at least 20 hours. A random sample of 30 smartphones shows a mean battery life of 18 hours with a standard deviation of 4 hours. At a significance level of 0.05, is there enough evidence to support the claim that the average battery life is less than 20 hours? Test this claim using the critical region technique, p-value technique, and confidence interval technique.

Solution 2​

Hypotheses & Given Parameters​
  • Null Hypothesis (H0H_0H0​): μ≥20 hours\mu \ge 20\text{ hours}μ≥20 hours
  • Alternative Hypothesis (HaH_aHa​): μ<20 hours\mu < 20\text{ hours}μ<20 hours (Left-tailed test, α=0.05\alpha = 0.05α=0.05)
  • Parameters: μ0=20 h, xˉ=18 h, s=4 h, n=30  ⟹  df=30−1=29\mu_0 = 20\text{ h}, \, \bar{x} = 18\text{ h}, \, s = 4\text{ h}, \, n = 30 \implies df = 30 - 1 = \mathbf{29}μ0​=20 h,xˉ=18 h,s=4 h,n=30⟹df=30−1=29
Standard Error & Test Statistic​

SE=sn=430≈0.7303 hoursSE = \frac{s}{\sqrt{n}} = \frac{4}{\sqrt{30}} \approx \mathbf{0.7303\text{ hours}}SE=n​s​=30​4​≈0.7303 hours tcal=xˉ−μ0SE=18−200.7303≈−2.74t_{\text{cal}} = \frac{\bar{x} - \mu_0}{SE} = \frac{18 - 20}{0.7303} \approx \mathbf{-2.74}tcal​=SExˉ−μ0​​=0.730318−20​≈−2.74

Method 1: Critical Region Technique​
  • Critical Value: tcrit=−1.699t_{\text{crit}} = \mathbf{-1.699}tcrit​=−1.699 (df=29,α=0.05df = 29, \alpha = 0.05df=29,α=0.05)
  • Rejection Rule: Reject H0H_0H0​ if tcal<−1.699t_{\text{cal}} < -1.699tcal​<−1.699.
  • Decision: tcal=−2.74<−1.699  ⟹  t_{\text{cal}} = -2.74 < -1.699 \impliestcal​=−2.74<−1.699⟹ Reject H0H_0H0​. Average battery life is significantly less than 20 hours (α=0.05\alpha = 0.05α=0.05).
Method 2: P-Value Technique​
  • Calculation: p-value=P(t29<−2.74)≈0.0052p\text{-value} = P(t_{29} < -2.74) \approx \mathbf{0.0052}p-value=P(t29​<−2.74)≈0.0052
  • Decision: p-value=0.0052≤0.05  ⟹  p\text{-value} = 0.0052 \le 0.05 \impliesp-value=0.0052≤0.05⟹ Reject H0H_0H0​.
Method 3: Confidence Interval Technique​
  • 95% Upper Bound: xˉ+(tα, df×SE)=18+(1.699×0.7303)=19.24 hours\bar{x} + (t_{\alpha, \, df} \times SE) = 18 + (1.699 \times 0.7303) = \mathbf{19.24\text{ hours}}xˉ+(tα,df​×SE)=18+(1.699×0.7303)=19.24 hours
  • Decision: Claimed 20 hours>19.24 hours20\text{ hours} > 19.24\text{ hours}20 hours>19.24 hours (lies above the 95% upper limit)   ⟹  \implies⟹ Reject H0H_0H0​.

info

Question Statement

A health organization states that the average daily intake of water for adults should be at least 3 liters. However, in a study of 40 adults, the average daily intake was found to be 2.5 liters with a standard deviation of 0.8 liters. At a significance level of 0.01, can we conclude that the average daily intake of water is less than the recommended 3 liters? Use the critical region technique, p-value technique, and confidence interval technique to support your answer.

Hypotheses & Given Parameters​

SE=sn=0.840≈0.1265 litersSE = \frac{s}{\sqrt{n}} = \frac{0.8}{\sqrt{40}} \approx \mathbf{0.1265\text{ liters}}SE=n​s​=40​0.8​≈0.1265 liters tcal=xˉ−μ0SE=2.5−3.00.1265≈−3.95t_{\text{cal}} = \frac{\bar{x} - \mu_0}{SE} = \frac{2.5 - 3.0}{0.1265} \approx \mathbf{-3.95}tcal​=SExˉ−μ0​​=0.12652.5−3.0​≈−3.95

Method 2: P-Value Technique​

info

Question Statement

A bakery claims that their average weight of a dozen cookies is 300 grams. To test this claim, a quality control manager randomly weighs 20 batches of cookies and finds that the mean weight is 295 grams with a standard deviation of 10 grams. At a 0.05 significance level, is there evidence to suggest that the average weight of the cookies is the same as 300 grams? . Use the (critical region technique, p-value technique, and confidence interval) any one technique to support your answer.

Solution 4​

Hypotheses & Parameters​
  • H0:μ=300 gH_0: \mu = 300\text{ g}H0​:μ=300 g vs Ha:μ≠300 gH_a: \mu \neq 300\text{ g}Ha​:μ=300 g (Two-tailed, α=0.05\alpha = 0.05α=0.05)
  • μ0=300 g, xˉ=295 g, s=10 g, n=20  ⟹  df=19\mu_0 = 300\text{ g}, \, \bar{x} = 295\text{ g}, \, s = 10\text{ g}, \, n = 20 \implies df = \mathbf{19}μ0​=300 g,xˉ=295 g,s=10 g,n=20⟹df=19
  • SE=1020≈2.2361 gSE = \frac{10}{\sqrt{20}} \approx \mathbf{2.2361\text{ g}}SE=20​10​≈2.2361 g
Technique Selected: Confidence Interval Technique​
  • Critical Value (tct_ctc​): t0.025,19=2.0930t_{0.025, 19} = \mathbf{2.0930}t0.025,19​=2.0930
  • Margin of Error: ME=2.0930×2.2361≈4.68 gME = 2.0930 \times 2.2361 \approx \mathbf{4.68\text{ g}}ME=2.0930×2.2361≈4.68 g
  • 95% CI: 295±4.68=[290.32 g, 299.68 g]295 \pm 4.68 = \mathbf{[290.32\text{ g}, \, 299.68\text{ g}]}295±4.68=[290.32 g,299.68 g]
  • Decision: Since 300 g∉[290.32,299.68]  ⟹  300\text{ g} \notin [290.32, 299.68] \implies300 g∈/[290.32,299.68]⟹ Reject H0H_0H0​. Average cookie weight is significantly different from 300g (underweight).
