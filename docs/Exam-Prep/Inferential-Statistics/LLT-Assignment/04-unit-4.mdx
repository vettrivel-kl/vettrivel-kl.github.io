## Question 1

:::info

**Question Statement**

A beverage company claims that the average sugar content in their new energy drink is 15 grams per can with a standard deviation of 3 grams. A random sample of 50 cans is tested, and the sample mean sugar content is found to be 14 grams. Is there sufficient evidence at the 0.05 significance level to conclude that the mean sugar content is different from 15 grams? Use the critical region technique, p-value technique, and confidence interval technique to test the claim.

:::

---

### Solution 1

##### Hypotheses & Given Parameters
*   **Null Hypothesis ($H_0$):** $\mu = 15\text{ g}$
*   **Alternative Hypothesis ($H_a$):** $\mu \neq 15\text{ g}$ (Two-tailed test, $\alpha = 0.05 \implies \alpha/2 = 0.025$)
*   **Parameters:** $\mu_0 = 15\text{ g}, \, \bar{x} = 14\text{ g}, \, \sigma = 3\text{ g}, \, n = 50\text{ cans}$ ($n \ge 30 \implies Z$-test)

##### Standard Error & Test Statistic
$$SE = \frac{\sigma}{\sqrt{n}} = \frac{3}{\sqrt{50}} \approx \mathbf{0.4243\text{ g}}$$
$$Z_{\text{cal}} = \frac{\bar{x} - \mu_0}{SE} = \frac{14 - 15}{0.4243} \approx \mathbf{-2.36}$$

---

*   **Critical Values:** $Z_{\text{crit}} = \mathbf{\pm 1.96}$
*   **Rejection Rule:** Reject $H_0$ if $Z_{\text{cal}} < -1.96$ or $Z_{\text{cal}} > +1.96$.

---

##### Method 2: P-Value Technique
*   **Calculation:** $p\text{-value} = 2 \times P(Z < -2.36) = 2 \times 0.0091 = \mathbf{0.0182}$
*   **Decision:** $p\text{-value} = 0.0182 \le 0.05 \implies$ **Reject $H_0$**. The difference is statistically significant.

---

##### Method 3: Confidence Interval Technique
*   **95% CI:** $\bar{x} \pm (1.96 \times SE) = 14 \pm (1.96 \times 0.4243) = 14 \pm 0.8316 = \mathbf{[13.17\text{ g}, \, 14.83\text{ g}]}$
*   **Decision:** The claimed mean of $15\text{ g}$ lies outside the interval ($15 \notin [13.17, 14.83]$) $\implies$ **Reject $H_0$**.

## Question 2

:::info

**Question Statement**

An electronics manufacturer claims that the average battery life of their latest smartphone model is at least 20 hours. A random sample of 30 smartphones shows a mean battery life of 18 hours with a standard deviation of 4 hours. At a significance level of 0.05, is there enough evidence to support the claim that the average battery life is less than 20 hours? Test this claim using the critical region technique, p-value technique, and confidence interval technique.

:::

---

### Solution 2

##### Hypotheses & Given Parameters
*   **Null Hypothesis ($H_0$):** $\mu \ge 20\text{ hours}$
*   **Alternative Hypothesis ($H_a$):** $\mu < 20\text{ hours}$ (Left-tailed test, $\alpha = 0.05$)
*   **Parameters:** $\mu_0 = 20\text{ h}, \, \bar{x} = 18\text{ h}, \, s = 4\text{ h}, \, n = 30 \implies df = 30 - 1 = \mathbf{29}$

##### Standard Error & Test Statistic
$$SE = \frac{s}{\sqrt{n}} = \frac{4}{\sqrt{30}} \approx \mathbf{0.7303\text{ hours}}$$
$$t_{\text{cal}} = \frac{\bar{x} - \mu_0}{SE} = \frac{18 - 20}{0.7303} \approx \mathbf{-2.74}$$

---

##### Method 1: Critical Region Technique
*   **Critical Value:** $t_{\text{crit}} = \mathbf{-1.699}$ ($df = 29, \alpha = 0.05$)
*   **Rejection Rule:** Reject $H_0$ if $t_{\text{cal}} < -1.699$.

## Question 3

:::info

**Question Statement**

A health organization states that the average daily intake of water for adults should be at least 3 liters. However, in a study of 40 adults, the average daily intake was found to be 2.5 liters with a standard deviation of 0.8 liters. At a significance level of 0.01, can we conclude that the average daily intake of water is less than the recommended 3 liters? Use the critical region technique, p-value technique, and confidence interval technique to support your answer.

:::

---

*   **Null Hypothesis ($H_0$):** $\mu \ge 3.0\text{ liters}$
