1. Conceptual Foundation: Why One-Way ANOVA Instead of Multiple t-Tests?​

If we compared the three methods using paired two-sample ttt-tests (AAA vs. BBB, BBB vs. CCC, and AAA vs. CCC), we would have to conduct (32)=3\binom{3}{2} = 3(23​)=3 separate hypothesis tests.

  • The Problem of Alpha Inflation (α\alphaα-inflation): For each test conducted at α=0.05\alpha = 0.05α=0.05, the probability of not committing a Type I error is 1−0.05=0.951 - 0.05 = 0.951−0.05=0.95.   • The ANOVA Solution: One-Way ANOVA performs an omnibus test that tests the equality of all three group means simultaneously in a single statistical test, rigorously maintaining the Type I error rate at exactly α=0.05\alpha = 0.05α=0.05.

6. Critical Assumptions & Common Misconceptions​

  1. Linearity Assumption: Pearson's rrr exclusively measures linear relationships. A curved (e.g., U-shaped or logarithmic) relationship can have r≈0r \approx 0r≈0 even if a perfect deterministic relationship exists.
  2. Extreme Outlier Sensitivity: Because Pearson's rrr uses squared deviations, a single extreme outlier can artificially inflate or destroy a correlation coefficient.
  3. Correlation Does NOT Imply Causation: While study hours and exam scores are strongly correlated, correlation alone does not prove that studying causes higher scores. Confounding variables—such as prior subject aptitude, intrinsic motivation, and access to learning resources—may simultaneously drive both study duration and academic performance.

7. Methodological Note: Interpolation vs. Extrapolation​

  • Interpolation (1≤X≤51 \le X \le 51≤X≤5): Predictions within the observed data range (e.g., predicting sales for X=3.5X = 3.5X=3.5) are statistically robust.
  • Extrapolation (X=6X = 6X=6): Predicting beyond the sample boundary assumes that the linear trend continues indefinitely. In real-world market economics, advertising eventually experiences diminishing marginal returns (saturation curve), meaning linear extrapolation should only be applied close to the observed boundary.
