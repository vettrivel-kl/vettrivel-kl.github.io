Question 4: Successive Draws (Replacement vs. Non-Replacement)​

Given Data​

A bag contains:

  • Red balls (RRR): 333
  • Black balls (BBB): 666
  • Total balls (NNN): 999

Let R1R_1R1​ denote the event that the first ball drawn is red, and R2R_2R2​ denote the event that the second ball drawn is red.

Step-by-Step Derivation​

(i) Case 1: With Replacement​

If the first ball is replaced before the second draw, the trials are independent. The composition of the bag remains unchanged for both draws.

  • Probability of first red ball: P(R1)=39=13P(R_1) = \frac{3}{9} = \frac{1}{3}P(R1​)=93​=31​
  • Since the ball is replaced, the conditional probability of the second draw is equal to its marginal probability: P(R2∣R1)=P(R2)=39=13P(R_2 \mid R_1) = P(R_2) = \frac{3}{9} = \frac{1}{3}P(R2​∣R1​)=P(R2​)=93​=31​
  • Joint probability of drawing two red balls in succession: P(R1∩R2)=P(R1)⋅P(R2∣R1)=13×13=19≈0.1111P(R_1 \cap R_2) = P(R_1) \cdot P(R_2 \mid R_1) = \frac{1}{3} \times \frac{1}{3} = \frac{1}{9} \approx 0.1111P(R1​∩R2​)=P(R1​)⋅P(R2​∣R1​)=31​×31​=91​≈0.1111

Inference: Under the independent trial condition (with replacement), the probability of drawing two red balls in succession is 1/9≈11.11%1/9 \approx 11.11\%1/9≈11.11%.

(ii) Case 2: Without Replacement​

If the first ball is not replaced, the trials are dependent. The composition of the bag for the second draw depends on the outcome of the first.

  • Probability of first red ball: P(R1)=39=13P(R_1) = \frac{3}{9} = \frac{1}{3}P(R1​)=93​=31​
  • After drawing a red ball, the remaining red balls are 222 and the total balls are 888: P(R2∣R1)=28=14P(R_2 \mid R_1) = \frac{2}{8} = \frac{1}{4}P(R2​∣R1​)=82​=41​
  • Joint probability of drawing two red balls in succession: P(R1∩R2)=P(R1)⋅P(R2∣R1)=13×14=112≈0.0833P(R_1 \cap R_2) = P(R_1) \cdot P(R_2 \mid R_1) = \frac{1}{3} \times \frac{1}{4} = \frac{1}{12} \approx 0.0833P(R1​∩R2​)=P(R1​)⋅P(R2​∣R1​)=31​×41​=121​≈0.0833

Inference: Under the dependent trial condition (without replacement), the probability of drawing two red balls in succession decreases to 1/12≈8.33%1/12 \approx 8.33\%1/12≈8.33% due to the depletion of the target class in the first draw.

Question 5: Drawing Red Balls Without Replacement​

Given Data​

A bag contains:

  • Red balls (RRR): 333
  • White balls (WWW): 444
  • Total balls (NNN): 777

We seek the joint probability of drawing two red balls in succession without replacement.

Step-by-Step Derivation​

Let R1R_1R1​ and R2R_2R2​ represent drawing a red ball on the first and second draws respectively.

  • Probability of drawing red on the first draw: P(R1)=37P(R_1) = \frac{3}{7}P(R1​)=73​
  • Since the draw is made without replacement, the conditional probability of drawing red on the second draw given that the first was red is: P(R2∣R1)=3−17−1=26=13P(R_2 \mid R_1) = \frac{3-1}{7-1} = \frac{2}{6} = \frac{1}{3}P(R2​∣R1​)=7−13−1​=62​=31​
  • Applying the multiplication rule for dependent events: P(R1∩R2)=P(R1)⋅P(R2∣R1)=37×13=17≈0.1429P(R_1 \cap R_2) = P(R_1) \cdot P(R_2 \mid R_1) = \frac{3}{7} \times \frac{1}{3} = \frac{1}{7} \approx 0.1429P(R1​∩R2​)=P(R1​)⋅P(R2​∣R1​)=73​×31​=71​≈0.1429

Inference: The probability that both drawn balls are red without replacement is 1/7≈14.29%1/7 \approx 14.29\%1/7≈14.29%.

Question 6: Bayes' Theorem and Bulbs from Selected Boxes​

Given Data​

Let B1B_1B1​ denote selecting Box 1, and B2B_2B2​ denote selecting Box 2. Since the box selection is random: P(B1)=P(B2)=0.5P(B_1) = P(B_2) = 0.5P(B1​)=P(B2​)=0.5

  • Box 1: 100010001000 bulbs total, 10%10\%10% defective (100100100 defective, 900900900 non-defective).
  • Box 2: 200020002000 bulbs total, 5%5\%5% defective (100100100 defective, 190019001900 non-defective).

Let DbothD_{both}Dboth​ denote the event that both drawn bulbs are defective (drawn without replacement).

Step-by-Step Derivation​

(i) Find the probability that both balls are defective (P(Dboth)P(D_{both})P(Dboth​))​

Using the Law of Total Probability: P(Dboth)=P(Dboth∣B1)P(B1)+P(Dboth∣B2)P(B2)P(D_{both}) = P(D_{both} \mid B_1)P(B_1) + P(D_{both} \mid B_2)P(B_2)P(Dboth​)=P(Dboth​∣B1​)P(B1​)+P(Dboth​∣B2​)P(B2​)

First, calculate the conditional probabilities of drawing two defective bulbs from each box:

  • From Box 1: P(Dboth∣B1)=1001000×99999=110×11111=111110≈0.009910P(D_{both} \mid B_1) = \frac{100}{1000} \times \frac{99}{999} = \frac{1}{10} \times \frac{11}{111} = \frac{11}{1110} \approx 0.009910P(Dboth​∣B1​)=1000100​×99999​=101​×11111​=111011​≈0.009910
  • From Box 2: P(Dboth∣B2)=1002000×991999=120×991999=9939980≈0.002476P(D_{both} \mid B_2) = \frac{100}{2000} \times \frac{99}{1999} = \frac{1}{20} \times \frac{99}{1999} = \frac{99}{39980} \approx 0.002476P(Dboth​∣B2​)=2000100​×199999​=201​×199999​=3998099​≈0.002476

Now, substitute these into the total probability formula: P(Dboth)=0.5×(111110)+0.5×(9939980)P(D_{both}) = 0.5 \times \left(\frac{11}{1110}\right) + 0.5 \times \left(\frac{99}{39980}\right)P(Dboth​)=0.5×(111011​)+0.5×(3998099​) P(Dboth)=112220+9979960=11×3998+99×1114437780×2P(D_{both}) = \frac{11}{2220} + \frac{99}{79960} = \frac{11 \times 3998 + 99 \times 111}{4437780 \times 2}P(Dboth​)=222011​+7996099​=4437780×211×3998+99×111​ Let's calculate decimals to preserve absolute clarity: P(Dboth)≈0.00495495+0.00123812=0.00619307P(D_{both}) \approx 0.00495495 + 0.00123812 = 0.00619307P(Dboth​)≈0.00495495+0.00123812=0.00619307 Or in exact fractional representation: P(Dboth)=549678875560≈0.0061931P(D_{both}) = \frac{54967}{8875560} \approx 0.0061931P(Dboth​)=887556054967​≈0.0061931

Inference: The total probability that both drawn bulbs are defective is approximately 0.619%0.619\%0.619%.

(ii) Assuming both are defective, find the probability they came from Box 1 (P(B1∣Dboth)P(B_1 \mid D_{both})P(B1​∣Dboth​))​

Using Bayes' Theorem: P(B1∣Dboth)=P(Dboth∣B1)P(B1)P(Dboth)P(B_1 \mid D_{both}) = \frac{P(D_{both} \mid B_1)P(B_1)}{P(D_{both})}P(B1​∣Dboth​)=P(Dboth​)P(Dboth​∣B1​)P(B1​)​ P(B1∣Dboth)=112220549678875560=112220×887556054967=11×399854967=4397854967≈0.80008P(B_1 \mid D_{both}) = \frac{\frac{11}{2220}}{\frac{54967}{8875560}} = \frac{11}{2220} \times \frac{8875560}{54967} = \frac{11 \times 3998}{54967} = \frac{43978}{54967} \approx 0.80008P(B1​∣Dboth​)=887556054967​222011​​=222011​×549678875560​=5496711×3998​=5496743978​≈0.80008

Inference: Given that both drawn bulbs are defective, there is an 80.01%80.01\%80.01% posterior probability that they originated from Box 1. This higher probability is due to Box 1 having a significantly higher density of defective items (10%10\%10%) than Box 2 (5%5\%5%).

Question 22: Drawing King or Queen (Addition Rule)​

Given Data​

A standard playing deck contains N=52N = 52N=52 cards.

  • Number of Kings (KKK) = 444
  • Number of Queens (QQQ) = 444

We seek the probability of drawing either a King or a Queen in a single draw (P(K∪Q)P(K \cup Q)P(K∪Q)).

Step-by-Step Derivation​

  • Probability of drawing a King: P(K)=452=113P(K) = \frac{4}{52} = \frac{1}{13}P(K)=524​=131​
  • Probability of drawing a Queen: P(Q)=452=113P(Q) = \frac{4}{52} = \frac{1}{13}P(Q)=524​=131​
  • Since a card cannot be both a King and a Queen simultaneously, these events are mutually exclusive: P(K∩Q)=0P(K \cap Q) = 0P(K∩Q)=0
  • Applying the general Addition Rule: P(K∪Q)=P(K)+P(Q)−P(K∩Q)=452+452−0=852=213≈0.1538P(K \cup Q) = P(K) + P(Q) - P(K \cap Q) = \frac{4}{52} + \frac{4}{52} - 0 = \frac{8}{52} = \frac{2}{13} \approx 0.1538P(K∪Q)=P(K)+P(Q)−P(K∩Q)=524​+524​−0=528​=132​≈0.1538

Inference: The probability of drawing a King or a Queen is 2/13≈15.38%2/13 \approx 15.38\%2/13≈15.38%.

Question 23: Club Membership Probability (Addition Rule)​

Given Data​

Let AAA denote membership in the Art Club, and MMM denote membership in the Music Club.

  • Probability of being in Art Club: P(A)=0.4P(A) = 0.4P(A)=0.4
  • Probability of being in Music Club: P(M)=0.5P(M) = 0.5P(M)=0.5
  • Probability of being in both clubs: P(A∩M)=0.2P(A \cap M) = 0.2P(A∩M)=0.2

We seek the probability of being a member of at least one club, i.e., P(A∪M)P(A \cup M)P(A∪M).

Step-by-Step Derivation​

Applying the General Addition Rule of Probability: P(A∪M)=P(A)+P(M)−P(A∩M)P(A \cup M) = P(A) + P(M) - P(A \cap M)P(A∪M)=P(A)+P(M)−P(A∩M) Substitute the given values into the equation: P(A∪M)=0.4+0.5−0.2=0.7P(A \cup M) = 0.4 + 0.5 - 0.2 = 0.7P(A∪M)=0.4+0.5−0.2=0.7

Inference: The probability that a randomly selected student is a member of at least one of the clubs is 0.700.700.70 (or 70%70\%70%). This accounts for the overlap of students who are members of both clubs, avoiding double-counting.
