One-Way ANOVA & F-Test

The definitive master guide to decomposing total variation into Treatment vs Error, testing $\ge 3$ independent group means simultaneously, avoiding $\alpha$-inflation, and constructing university-grade ANOVA tables.

01. Intuition: Partitioning of Total Variation

Invented by Sir Ronald A. Fisher, **ANOVA (Analysis of Variance)** tests whether $k \ge 3$ independent population means are equal ($H_0: \mu_1 = \mu_2 = \dots = \mu_k$) by decomposing the total dispersion in the dataset into two orthogonal components:

$$\begin{aligned} \text{Total Sum of Squares (TSS)} = \text{Between Treatments (SSB)} + \text{Within Groups Error (SSW)} \end{aligned}$$ Variance Ratio: $F = \frac{\text{Mean Square Between (Signal)}}{\text{Mean Square Within (Noise)}} = \frac{\text{MSB}}{\text{MSW}}$
Between Treatments · Treatment Effect

SSB / SSTr — The Signal

Measures how much the individual group sample means $\bar{X}_j$ deviate from the grand overall mean $\bar{X}$. Reflects true experimental differences plus sampling noise.

Within Groups · Random Residual

SSW / SSE — The Noise

Measures the internal variation of individual observations $X_{ij}$ around their own group mean $\bar{X}_j$. Reflects purely unexplained random experimental error.

The Core F-Ratio Decision Logic:
• If $H_0$ is true (no treatment effect), $\text{MSB} \approx \text{MSW} \implies F_{\text{calc}} \approx 1.0$.
• If $H_1$ is true (treatments create real differences), $\text{MSB} \gg \text{MSW} \implies F_{\text{calc}} \gg 1.0$.
• When $F_{\text{calc}} > F_{\text{critical}}(k-1, N-k, \alpha)$, we reject $H_0$.

02. Why ANOVA? The Family-Wise Error Problem

A mandatory 5-mark theory question on every semester exam asks: "Why can't we just run multiple two-sample t-tests?"

The Compound Probability Explosion

To compare $k$ group means using pairwise $t$-tests, the number of independent pairwise comparisons is:

$$\begin{aligned} C = \binom{k}{2} = \frac{k(k - 1)}{2} \end{aligned}$$

For $k = 4$ groups, $C = \binom{4}{2} = 6$ individual tests are required.

If each test is performed at significance level $\alpha = 0.05$, the probability of making at least one false positive (Type I error) across the entire experiment explodes into the **Family-Wise Error Rate ($\alpha_{\text{FW}}$)**:

$$\begin{aligned} \alpha_{\text{FW}} &= 1 - (1 - \alpha)^C \\[4pt] &= 1 - (0.95)^6 = 1 - 0.7351 = \mathbf{0.2649 \ (26.49\%)} \end{aligned}$$
The Statistical Disaster: With 4 groups, running pairwise $t$-tests means a 26.5% probability of declaring a fake significant difference purely by chance! ANOVA solves this by conducting a single omnibus $F$-test while strictly capping Type I error at $\alpha = 0.05$.

03. The 6-Step Computational Algorithm

Compute Group Totals & Grand Total ($G$)

Calculate column totals $T_j = \sum x_{ij}$. Then sum them for the Grand Total $G = \sum T_j$. Count total observations $N = \sum n_j$.

Compute the Correction Factor (CF)

$$\begin{aligned} \text{CF} = \frac{G^2}{N} \end{aligned}$$

Calculate Total Sum of Squares (TSS)

Square every single raw value in the table, sum them, and subtract CF:

$$\begin{aligned} \text{TSS} = \sum \sum X_{ij}^2 - \text{CF}, \quad df_{\text{total}} = N - 1 \end{aligned}$$

Calculate Between-Groups Sum of Squares (SSB)

Square each group total, divide by its group size $n_j$, sum them, and subtract CF:

$$\begin{aligned} \text{SSB} = \sum_{j=1}^k \frac{T_j^2}{n_j} - \text{CF}, \quad df_{\text{between}} = k - 1 \end{aligned}$$

Calculate Within-Groups Error Sum of Squares (SSW)

Obtain error SS by simple subtraction (guaranteed exact arithmetic):

$$\begin{aligned} \text{SSW} = \text{TSS} - \text{SSB}, \quad df_{\text{within}} = N - k \end{aligned}$$

Calculate Mean Squares & F-Ratio

$$\begin{aligned} \text{MSB} = \frac{\text{SSB}}{k - 1}, \quad \text{MSW} = \frac{\text{SSW}}{N - k} \implies F_{\text{calc}} = \frac{\text{MSB}}{\text{MSW}} \end{aligned}$$

05. F-Test for Equality of Two Variances

Used to test if two independent populations have identical variances ($H_0: \sigma_1^2 = \sigma_2^2$). Also serves as the test of homogeneity of variance before pooling $t$-tests.

$$\begin{aligned} F = \frac{S_1^2}{S_2^2}, \quad \text{where } S_1^2 \ge S_2^2 \end{aligned}$$ Degrees of freedom: Numerator $\nu_1 = n_1 - 1$, Denominator $\nu_2 = n_2 - 1$

Standard Sample Variance Formula:

$$\begin{aligned} S^2 = \frac{\sum (X - \bar{X})^2}{n - 1} = \frac{\sum X^2 - \frac{(\sum X)^2}{n}}{n - 1} \end{aligned}$$

Always place the larger sample variance in the numerator so that calculated $F \ge 1.0$. This allows direct comparison against standard right-tail $F$ tables.

06. 6 Deadly Exam Traps & Common Student Mistakes

Trap 1 · Forgetting to Subtract CF

Treating $\sum X^2$ as TSS

$\text{TSS} \ne \sum \sum X_{ij}^2$. You must subtract the Correction Factor: $\text{TSS} = \sum \sum X^2 - \text{CF}$. Forgetting CF inflates TSS by thousands!

Trap 2 · Wrong Divisor in SSB

Dividing $\sum T_j^2$ by $N$ instead of $n_j$

In $\text{SSB} = \sum \frac{T_j^2}{n_j} - \text{CF}$, the divisor is the group sample size $n_j$ (e.g. 5 or 10), NOT the total sample size $N$!

Trap 3 · Inverted Degrees of Freedom

Looking up $F(16, 3)$ instead of $F(3, 16)$

Numerator $df$ is always Treatment ($k - 1 = 3$). Denominator $df$ is Error ($N - k = 16$). Swapping them gives an incorrect critical cutoff.

Trap 4 · Over-Interpreting Omnibus F

Claiming ANOVA proves *which* pair differs

A significant $F$-test proves only that at least one pair differs. It does not pinpoint the specific pair without post-hoc tests (Tukey's HSD / Scheffe).

Trap 5 · Negative Sum of Squares

Producing a negative SSB or SSW

Sums of squares are squared deviations and can NEVER be negative. If $\text{SSB} < 0$, your CF or $\sum T_j^2 / n$ calculation has an arithmetic error.

Trap 6 · Degrees of Freedom Balance

Failing the Check: $df_B + df_W = df_{\text{total}}$

Always verify: $(k - 1) + (N - k) = N - 1$. For example, $3 + 16 = 19$, or $3 + 36 = 39$. If this sum fails, do not proceed to mean squares.

Question 1: Why ANOVA Differs from Multiple t-Tests (5 Marks)

Question Statement (Verbatim):
"Explain how ANOVA differs from t-tests in hypothesis testing. Discuss why ANOVA is preferable when comparing the means of more than two groups. (5 Marks)"

View Complete Theory Model Answer

1. Fundamental Differences:

DimensionTwo-Sample t-TestOne-Way ANOVA (F-Test)
Number of GroupsExactly two groups ($k = 2$)Two or more groups ($k \ge 3$)
Hypotheses$H_0: \mu_1 = \mu_2$$H_0: \mu_1 = \mu_2 = \dots = \mu_k$
Test StatisticStudent's $t$ (ratio of mean diff to SE)Fisher's $F$ (ratio of Between MS to Within MS)
Mathematical RelationFor $k = 2$, the $F$-test is mathematically identical to $t^2$: $F = t^2$.

2. Why ANOVA is Preferable: The Family-Wise Error Rate Problem:

When comparing $k = 4$ groups, conducting pairwise $t$-tests requires $C = \binom{4}{2} = 6$ individual tests. If each test is evaluated at $\alpha = 0.05$, the cumulative Type I error probability across the family of tests is:

$$\begin{aligned} \alpha_{\text{FW}} &= 1 - (1 - \alpha)^C \\[4pt] &= 1 - (0.95)^6 = 1 - 0.7351 = \mathbf{0.2649 \ (26.49\%)} \end{aligned}$$

Conclusion: Running multiple $t$-tests leads to an unacceptably high $26.5\%$ risk of finding false significance. ANOVA avoids this by testing all means simultaneously in a single test, strictly preserving the Type I error at $\alpha = 0.05$.

Question 5: Testing Equality of Two Population Variances

Question Statement:
"Two independent manufacturing machines produce steel rods. A sample of 10 rods from Machine A has sample variance $s_1^2 = 25.0$. A sample of 12 rods from Machine B has sample variance $s_2^2 = 10.0$. At $\alpha = 0.05$, test whether Machine A produces significantly more variable rod diameters than Machine B."

View Complete Step-by-Step Solution

1. Formulate Hypotheses:

  • Null Hypothesis: $H_0: \sigma_1^2 = \sigma_2^2$ (Both machines have identical variance).
  • Alternative Hypothesis: $H_1: \sigma_1^2 > \sigma_2^2$ (Right-tailed test).

2. Test Statistic & Degrees of Freedom:

$$\begin{aligned} F_{\text{calc}} = \frac{s_1^2}{s_2^2} = \frac{25.0}{10.0} = \mathbf{2.50} \end{aligned}$$
  • Numerator $df$: $\nu_1 = n_1 - 1 = 10 - 1 = \mathbf{9}$
  • Denominator $df$: $\nu_2 = n_2 - 1 = 12 - 1 = \mathbf{11}$

3. Critical Value Lookup & Decision:

From the standard $F$-distribution table for $\alpha = 0.05$ with $df = (9, 11)$:

$$\begin{aligned} F_{0.05}(9, 11) = \mathbf{2.90} \end{aligned}$$

Since $F_{\text{calc}} = 2.50 < F_{\text{crit}} = 2.90$, we **fail to reject $H_0$**.

Conclusion: There is insufficient statistical evidence at the $5\%$ level to conclude that Machine A produces significantly more variable rods than Machine B.

08. Interactive Self-Test Quiz (Active Recall)

Test your ANOVA degrees of freedom, F-ratio, and $\alpha$-inflation logic before proceeding. Attempt all 4 questions:

Question 1 — Family-Wise Error Rate ($\alpha_{\text{FW}}$)

If an experiment evaluates $k = 4$ group means using pairwise $t$-tests at $\alpha = 0.05$, how many pairwise tests are required, and what is the approximate Family-Wise Type I error rate?

Correct: 6 tests, $\alpha_{\text{FW}} \approx 26.5\%$.
$C = \binom{4}{2} = 6$. $\alpha_{\text{FW}} = 1 - (1 - 0.05)^6 = 1 - 0.7351 = 0.2649 = 26.49\%$. This illustrates why ANOVA is mandatory.
Question 2 — Degrees of Freedom Identity

A One-Way ANOVA is conducted on $k = 4$ iron formations with $n = 10$ samples in each group ($N = 40$). What are the Between-Groups and Within-Groups degrees of freedom?

Correct: $df_B = 3, df_W = 36$.
$df_{\text{between}} = k - 1 = 4 - 1 = 3$. $df_{\text{within}} = N - k = 40 - 4 = 36$. Check: $3 + 36 = 39 = N - 1$.
Question 3 — F-Test Statistical Decision

In a One-Way ANOVA, the calculated $F = 10.85$ and the critical table value is $F_{0.01}(3, 36) = 4.38$. What is the correct statistical decision?

Correct: Option A.
Since $F_{\text{calc}} = 10.85 > F_{\text{crit}} = 4.38$, the between-group variation is far larger than random error, rejecting $H_0$ decisively at $\alpha = 0.01$.
Question 4 — Meaning of $F \approx 1.0$

What does an ANOVA variance ratio of $F_{\text{calc}} \approx 1.0$ imply?

Correct: Option D.
When $\text{MSB} \approx \text{MSW}$, the differences between group means are entirely consistent with random chance, meaning treatments produced no measurable effect.

09. Exam Hall Answer Sheet Presentation Checklist

When solving an ANOVA problem in the semester exam, follow this exact structure to guarantee maximum marks:

  1. State the Hypotheses: $H_0: \mu_1 = \mu_2 = \dots = \mu_k$ versus $H_1: \text{At least one mean differs}$.
  2. Summary Table: Write group totals $T_j$, sample sizes $n_j$, and Grand Total $G$.
  3. Correction Factor: Write $\text{CF} = \frac{G^2}{N}$ and evaluate.
  4. Total SS: Write $\text{TSS} = \sum X^2 - \text{CF}$ with $df = N - 1$.
  5. Between SS: Write $\text{SSB} = \sum \frac{T_j^2}{n_j} - \text{CF}$ with $df = k - 1$.
  6. Within SS: Write $\text{SSW} = \text{TSS} - \text{SSB}$ with $df = N - k$.
  7. Draw Complete ANOVA Table: Present Source, SS, df, MS, and $F_{\text{calc}}$.
  8. Critical Value & Decision: State $F_{\text{crit}}(k-1, N-k, \alpha)$. State whether $F_{\text{calc}} > F_{\text{crit}}$ and write final English conclusion.
