Chi-Square Tests ($\chi^2$)

The comprehensive master guide covering contingency table independence, expected frequency matrices, Yates' continuity correction, distribution goodness-of-fit, and pooling rules.

01. Intuition: The Discrepancy Metric

Developed by Karl Pearson, the **Chi-Square ($\chi^2$) statistic** measures the normalized discrepancy between empirically observed counts ($O$) and theoretically expected counts ($E$ under $H_0$):

$$\begin{aligned} \chi^2 = \sum_{i=1}^k \frac{(O_i - E_i)^2}{E_i} \sim \chi^2(df) \end{aligned}$$ Sum of Squared Residuals Weighted by Theoretical Frequencies
Core Test 1 · Cross-Tabulation

Test of Independence

Determines if two categorical attributes (e.g., Treatment vs Recovery, Smoker vs Lung Cancer) are statistically independent in an $r \times c$ contingency table.

Core Test 2 · Theoretical Conformity

Goodness of Fit Test

Evaluates whether an observed sample frequency distribution conforms to a theoretical probability model (Uniform, Poisson, Binomial, Mendelian genetic ratios).

4 Essential Conditions for Validity (University Rubric):
1. **Total Sample Size:** $N \ge 50$.
2. **Minimum Expected Frequency (Rule of 5):** No expected cell frequency $E_i$ should be $< 5$. If $E_i < 5$, pool adjacent classes or apply Yates' correction.
3. **Raw Counts Only:** Observations MUST be raw counts/frequencies. Never apply $\chi^2$ directly to percentages, proportions, or averages.
4. **Mutually Exclusive & Random:** Each subject must belong to exactly one category, sampled independently.

02. Contingency Tables & Test of Independence

In an $r \times c$ contingency table with $r$ rows and $c$ columns, the expected frequency for cell $(i, j)$ under $H_0$ (Independence) is:

$$\begin{aligned} E_{ij} = \frac{(\text{Row } i \text{ Total}) \times (\text{Column } j \text{ Total})}{\text{Grand Total } N} \end{aligned}$$ Degrees of Freedom: $df = (r - 1)(c - 1)$

Direct $2 \times 2$ Table Shortcut Formula

For a standard $2 \times 2$ table $\begin{pmatrix} a & b \\ c & d \end{pmatrix}$ with $N = a + b + c + d$, Pearson's statistic simplifies without calculating individual $E_{ij}$:

$$\begin{aligned} \chi^2 = \frac{N (ad - bc)^2}{(a + b)(c + d)(a + c)(b + d)}, \quad df = (2 - 1)(2 - 1) = 1 \end{aligned}$$

03. Yates' Correction for Continuity ($2 \times 2$ Table)

Because the discrete counts are approximated by a continuous $\chi^2$ distribution, Frank Yates introduced a continuity correction ($\pm 0.5$) for $2 \times 2$ tables, especially when cell counts are modest:

$$\begin{aligned} \chi_{\text{Yates}}^2 = \frac{N \left(|ad - bc| - \frac{N}{2}\right)^2}{(a + b)(c + d)(a + c)(b + d)}, \quad df = 1 \end{aligned}$$ Subtracting $N/2$ contracts the discrepancy towards zero, preventing Type I error inflation
Examiner's Grading Rule: Whenever solving a $2 \times 2$ table in Section B or C, presenting **both** the uncorrected Pearson $\chi^2$ and Yates' continuity corrected $\chi^2$ guarantees 100% marks under any answer key!

04. Goodness of Fit & Parameter Estimation Rules

Tests whether an observed frequency distribution $O_i$ matches expected frequencies $E_i = N \cdot P(X = x_i)$.

Degrees of Freedom in Goodness of Fit:

$$\begin{aligned} df = k - 1 - m \end{aligned}$$
  • $k$: Number of categories/classes after pooling small cells ($E < 5$).
  • $1$: Subtracted because $\sum E_i = \sum O_i = N$ (Total frequency constraint).
  • $m$: Number of population parameters estimated from the sample data:
    • $m = 0$ for completely specified theoretical ratios (e.g. Mendelian 9:3:3:1 or Fair Die $1/6$).
    • $m = 1$ when estimating mean rate $\lambda = \bar{x}$ (Poisson) or success rate $p$ (Binomial).
    • $m = 2$ when estimating mean $\mu$ and standard deviation $\sigma$ (Normal).

05. 6 Deadly Exam Traps & Common Student Mistakes

Trap 1 · Observed in Denominator

Dividing by $O$ instead of $E$

The denominator MUST be Expected frequency: $\frac{(O - E)^2}{E}$. Writing $\frac{(O - E)^2}{O}$ is a fatal conceptual error.

Trap 2 · Ignoring $E < 5$ Pooling Rule

Testing cells where expected frequency is $< 5$

If any expected cell has $E_i < 5$, you MUST pool it with an adjacent class. Failing to pool invalidates the large-sample $\chi^2$ approximation.

Trap 3 · Wrong Degrees of Freedom

Using $df = N - 1$ on a Contingency Table

In contingency tables, $df = (r - 1)(c - 1)$, NOT $N - 1$. For a $2 \times 2$ table, $df = 1$. For a $3 \times 3$ table, $df = (2)(2) = 4$.

Trap 4 · Forgetting $m$ in Goodness of Fit

Using $df = k - 1$ when $\lambda$ was estimated

When you compute sample mean $\lambda = \sum fx / N$, you used up 1 degree of freedom! The correct $df$ is $k - 1 - 1 = k - 2$.

Trap 5 · Testing Proportions Directly

Applying $\chi^2$ to percentages like 40% and 60%

$\chi^2$ requires integer frequencies. Always multiply percentages by sample size to get raw headcounts ($O$) before computing $\chi^2$.

Trap 6 · Two-Tailed Critical Lookups

Dividing $\alpha$ by 2

$\chi^2$ is inherently a **one-tailed (right-tailed) test** because deviations are squared. For $\alpha = 0.05$ with $df = 1$, use $\chi^2_{0.05, 1} = \mathbf{3.841}$.

Question 1: Epidemic Disease Treatment Effectiveness ($2 \times 2$ Table) (10 Marks)

Question Statement (Verbatim):
"In an epidemic of a certain disease, 92 children contracted the disease. Of these, 41 received no treatment and of these 10 showed after-effects. Of the remainder who did receive treatment, 17 showed after-effects. Test the hypothesis that the treatment was not effective at $\alpha = 0.05$. (10 Marks)"

View Complete Step-by-Step Solution (Pearson & Yates)

1. Construct the $2 \times 2$ Contingency Table:

Total children $N = 92$. Untreated = 41 (10 after-effects, $41 - 10 = 31$ no after-effects). Treated = $92 - 41 = 51$ (17 after-effects, $51 - 17 = 34$ no after-effects).

GroupAfter-EffectsNo After-EffectsRow Total
No Treatment$a = 10$$b = 31$$a + b = \mathbf{41}$
Treatment$c = 17$$d = 34$$c + d = \mathbf{51}$
Col Total$a + c = \mathbf{27}$$b + d = \mathbf{65}$$N = \mathbf{92}$

2. Cross-Product Term:

$$\begin{aligned} ad - bc &= (10 \times 34) - (31 \times 17) = 340 - 527 = \mathbf{-187} \\[4pt] (ad - bc)^2 &= (-187)^2 = \mathbf{34,969} \end{aligned}$$

3. Uncorrected Pearson Chi-Square ($\chi^2$):

$$\begin{aligned} \chi^2 &= \frac{N (ad - bc)^2}{(a + b)(c + d)(a + c)(b + d)} \\[6pt] &= \frac{92 \times 34,969}{41 \times 51 \times 27 \times 65} = \frac{3,217,148}{3,668,535} \approx \mathbf{0.8769} \end{aligned}$$

4. With Yates' Continuity Correction:

$$\begin{aligned} |ad - bc| - \frac{N}{2} &= |-187| - \frac{92}{2} = 187 - 46 = \mathbf{141} \\[4pt] \chi_{\text{Yates}}^2 &= \frac{92 \times (141)^2}{3,668,535} = \frac{92 \times 19,881}{3,668,535} = \frac{1,829,052}{3,668,535} \approx \mathbf{0.4986} \end{aligned}$$
Decision & Conclusion:
Degrees of freedom: $df = (2 - 1)(2 - 1) = \mathbf{1}$. Critical value at $\alpha = 0.05$ is $\chi^2_{0.05, 1} = \mathbf{3.841}$.
Since $\chi^2_{\text{calc}} = 0.8769 < 3.841$ (and $\chi^2_{\text{Yates}} = 0.4986 < 3.841$), we fail to reject $H_0$.
There is no statistically significant evidence at the 5% level to show that the treatment was effective in reducing after-effects.

4 Methodological Uses of the Chi-Square Test (Full Marks Criteria):

  1. Test of Independence of Attributes: Evaluates whether two qualitative variables in a contingency table ($r \times c$) possess an association or operate independently ($df = (r-1)(c-1)$).
  2. Test of Goodness of Fit: Assesses whether observed empirical frequencies conform to a hypothesized theoretical distribution (Poisson, Binomial, Normal, Uniform) ($df = k - 1 - m$).
  3. Test of Homogeneity: Tests whether multiple independent sub-populations are homogeneous with respect to a specific categorical characteristic.
  4. Test of a Single Population Variance: Tests whether a sample variance $s^2$ from a normal population conforms to a hypothesized population variance $\sigma_0^2$ via the test statistic: $$\begin{aligned} \chi^2 = \frac{(n - 1)s^2}{\sigma_0^2} \sim \chi^2(n - 1) \end{aligned}$$

  • Adult: $E_{11} = \frac{170 \times 160}{390} = 69.23; \; E_{12} = \frac{170 \times 150}{390} = 64.62; \; E_{13} = \frac{170 \times 80}{390} = 36.15$
  • Teen: $E_{21} = \frac{110 \times 160}{390} = 44.87; \; E_{22} = \frac{110 \times 150}{390} = 41.87; \; E_{23} = \frac{110 \times 80}{390} = 23.41$
  • Child: $E_{31} = 44.87; \; E_{32} = 41.87; \; E_{33} = 23.41$

2. Chi-Square Summation:

$$\begin{aligned} \chi^2 &= \frac{(80 - 69.23)^2}{69.23} + \frac{(50 - 64.62)^2}{64.62} + \dots + \frac{(70 - 23.41)^2}{23.41} \\[4pt] &= 1.676 + 3.308 + 0.410 + 4.928 + 7.850 + 0.497 + 0.529 + 3.366 + 92.74 \\[4pt] &\approx \mathbf{115.30} \end{aligned}$$

Question 5: Testing a Fair 6-Sided Die (Discrete Uniform Fit)

Question Statement:
"A six-sided die is rolled 120 times with observed outcomes: 1: 15, 2: 24, 3: 18, 4: 21, 5: 25, 6: 17. At $\alpha = 0.05$, test whether the die is fair."

View Complete Step-by-Step Solution

1. Theoretical Expected Frequencies: Under $H_0$ (fair die), each face has probability $1/6$.

$$\begin{aligned} E_i = 120 \times \frac{1}{6} = \mathbf{20} \quad \text{for all } i \in \{1, 2, 3, 4, 5, 6\} \end{aligned}$$

2. Compute Chi-Square Statistic:

$$\begin{aligned} \chi^2 &= \sum_{i=1}^6 \frac{(O_i - 20)^2}{20} \\[4pt] &= \frac{(15-20)^2 + (24-20)^2 + (18-20)^2 + (21-20)^2 + (25-20)^2 + (17-20)^2}{20} \\[4pt] &= \frac{25 + 16 + 4 + 1 + 25 + 9}{20} = \frac{80}{20} = \mathbf{4.00} \end{aligned}$$

3. Degrees of Freedom & Decision:

$$\begin{aligned} df &= k - 1 - 0 = 6 - 1 = \mathbf{5} \\[4pt] \chi^2_{0.05, 5} &= \mathbf{11.070} \end{aligned}$$
Since $\chi^2_{\text{calc}} = 4.00 < 11.070$, we **fail to reject $H_0$**.
The die is consistent with a fair, balanced six-sided die.

08. Interactive Self-Test Quiz (Active Recall)

Test your contingency table, Yates' correction, and goodness of fit skills. Attempt all 4 questions:

Question 1 — Contingency Table Degrees of Freedom

In a study with a $3 \times 4$ contingency table, what are the degrees of freedom for testing independence?

Correct: $df = (r - 1)(c - 1) = (3 - 1)(4 - 1) = 2 \times 3 = \mathbf{6}$.
Question 2 — Purpose of Yates' Continuity Correction

Why is Yates' correction applied to a $2 \times 2$ contingency table?

Correct: Option A.
Subtracting $N/2$ contracts the discrepancy towards zero, correcting for continuity and reducing Type I error inflation.
Question 3 — Rule of 5 Pooling Requirement

In a Chi-square test, what must be done if an expected frequency is $E_i = 3.2$?

Correct: Option C.
Under Cochran's criteria, every expected cell frequency must be $\ge 5$. Small cells are pooled, reducing the number of effective classes $k$.
Question 4 — Poisson Goodness of Fit Degrees of Freedom

A sample of counts is classified into 7 categories after pooling, and the Poisson mean $\lambda$ is estimated from the data. What is the degrees of freedom?

Correct: $df = k - 1 - m = 7 - 1 - 1 = \mathbf{5}$.
One degree of freedom is lost for total frequency constraint, and another is lost for estimating $\lambda$.

09. Exam Hall Answer Sheet Presentation Checklist

When solving a Chi-Square problem in the semester exam, follow this exact structure to guarantee maximum marks:

  1. State the Hypotheses: $H_0$: The two attributes are independent (or data follows specified distribution) vs $H_1$: Dependent.
  2. Contingency Table with Marginals: Draw the table with Row Totals, Column Totals, and Grand Total $N$.
  3. Expected Frequencies: Write $E_{ij} = \frac{R_i \times C_j}{N}$ and list all cell expectations.
  4. Check the Rule of 5: Explicitly note: "All expected frequencies $E_i \ge 5$, so Chi-square conditions are satisfied."
  5. Chi-Square Calculation: State formula $\chi^2 = \sum \frac{(O-E)^2}{E}$ (and Yates formula for $2 \times 2$). Show arithmetic table.
  6. Degrees of Freedom & Critical Value: State $df = (r-1)(c-1)$ or $df = k - 1 - m$ and look up $\chi^2_{\alpha, df}$.
  7. Final Decision & Contextual Conclusion: Compare $\chi^2_{\text{calc}}$ against $\chi^2_{\text{crit}}$ and write an unambiguous conclusion sentence.
