02. Master Test Selection Matrix: "Which Test Do I Use?"

In the semester examination, marks depend first on selecting the correct statistical test. Use this decision reference based on sample sizes, known variances, and data types:

Scenario / Data Type Conditions & Constraints Selected Test Test Statistic Formula Degrees of Freedom ($df$)
One Sample Mean ($\mu$) Population $\sigma$ known OR $n \ge 30$ One-Sample Z-Test $Z = \frac{\bar{x} - \mu_0}{\sigma / \sqrt{n}}$ N/A (Standard Normal)
One Sample Mean ($\mu$) Population $\sigma$ unknown AND $n < 30$ (Normal pop) One-Sample t-Test $t = \frac{\bar{x} - \mu_0}{s / \sqrt{n}}$ $df = n - 1$
Two Independent Means ($\mu_1 - \mu_2$) $\sigma_1, \sigma_2$ known OR $n_1, n_2 \ge 30$ Two-Sample Z-Test $Z = \frac{\bar{x}_1 - \bar{x}_2}{\sqrt{\frac{\sigma_1^2}{n_1} + \frac{\sigma_2^2}{n_2}}}$ N/A (Standard Normal)
Two Independent Means ($\mu_1 - \mu_2$) $\sigma_1 = \sigma_2$ unknown, $n_1, n_2 < 30$ Independent Pooled t-Test $t = \frac{\bar{x}_1 - \bar{x}_2}{s_p \sqrt{\frac{1}{n_1} + \frac{1}{n_2}}}$ $df = n_1 + n_2 - 2$
Paired Samples (Before & After) Same subjects measured twice; differences $d_i = x_{1i} - x_{2i}$ Paired t-Test $t = \frac{\bar{d}}{s_d / \sqrt{n}}$ $df = n - 1$
One Proportion ($P$) Single categorical sample, testing against hypothesized $P_0$ One-Proportion Z-Test $Z = \frac{p - P_0}{\sqrt{\frac{P_0(1-P_0)}{n}}}$ N/A (Standard Normal)
Two Independent Proportions ($P_1 - P_2$) Two independent categorical groups Two-Proportion Z-Test $Z = \frac{p_1 - p_2}{\sqrt{\bar{p}\bar{q}\left(\frac{1}{n_1} + \frac{1}{n_2}\right)}}$ $\bar{p} = \frac{x_1 + x_2}{n_1 + n_2}$ (pooled)

03. Priority 1: Bayes' Theorem & Total Probability

Dedicated Deep Dive Available: Module 01
Contains all 7 past university exam problems fully solved, 6 deadly exam traps, and an interactive 4-question active recall quiz.
Open Module 01 Guide →

Core Concept: Bayes' Theorem performs an inversion of conditional probability. It computes the posterior probability of a hidden cause $B_i$ given that an observable event / symptom $A$ has occurred.

$$\begin{aligned} P(B_i \mid A) &= \frac{P(B_i) \cdot P(A \mid B_i)}{P(A)} \\[4pt] &= \frac{P(B_i) \cdot P(A \mid B_i)}{\sum_{j=1}^k P(B_j) \cdot P(A \mid B_j)} \end{aligned}$$
The 4 Standard Terms:
  • Prior Probability $P(B_i)$: Initial probability of machine/source $B_i$ before observing evidence.
  • Likelihood $P(A \mid B_i)$: Probability that source $B_i$ generates defective item $A$.
  • Total Probability $P(A)$: Marginal probability of observing $A$ across all sources: $P(A) = \sum P(B_j) P(A \mid B_j)$.
  • Posterior Probability $P(B_i \mid A)$: Updated probability that source $B_i$ was the origin of defective item $A$.
Solved Sem Exam Question 1 (May 2025, Q2 - 20 Marks): Box Bulb Defect Problem

Problem Statement: Box 1 contains 1000 bulbs of which 10% are defective. Box 2 contains 2000 bulbs of which 5% are defective. Two bulbs are drawn (without replacement) from a randomly selected box.
(i) Find the probability that both bulbs are defective.
(ii) Assuming that both are defective, find the probability that they came from Box 1.

Step 1: Event Definitions & Priors: $P(B_1) = 0.5, P(B_2) = 0.5$.

Step 2: Likelihoods (Sampling Without Replacement):

  • Box 1 ($N_1 = 1000$, Def = $100$): $$\begin{aligned} P(D \mid B_1) &= \frac{\binom{100}{2}}{\binom{1000}{2}} = \frac{100 \times 99}{1000 \times 999} = \frac{11}{1110} \approx \mathbf{0.009910} \end{aligned}$$
  • Box 2 ($N_2 = 2000$, Def = $100$): $$\begin{aligned} P(D \mid B_2) &= \frac{\binom{100}{2}}{\binom{2000}{2}} = \frac{100 \times 99}{2000 \times 1999} = \frac{99}{39980} \approx \mathbf{0.002476} \end{aligned}$$

Step 3: Part (i) Total Probability $P(D)$:

$$\begin{aligned} P(D) &= 0.5(0.009910) + 0.5(0.002476) = 0.004955 + 0.001238 = \mathbf{0.006193 \ (0.6193\%)} \end{aligned}$$

Step 4: Part (ii) Posterior Probability $P(B_1 \mid D)$:

$$\begin{aligned} P(B_1 \mid D) &= \frac{0.004955}{0.006193} = \frac{3,998}{4,997} \approx \mathbf{0.8001 \ (80.01\%)} \end{aligned}$$

04. Priority 2: Normal Distribution & Central Limit Theorem

Dedicated Deep Dive Available: Module 02

Open Module 02 Guide →

Key Properties of Gaussian Curve:

  • PDF: $f(x) = \frac{1}{\sigma \sqrt{2\pi}} e^{-\frac{1}{2}\left(\frac{x-\mu}{\sigma}\right)^2}, \quad -\infty < x < \infty$.
  • Symmetric bell curve centered at $\mu = \text{median} = \text{mode}$. Total area $= 1.0$.
  • Inflection points occur at $x = \mu \pm \sigma$. Skewness $\beta_1 = 0$; Kurtosis $\beta_2 = 3$ (mesokurtic).
  • Empirical Rule: $P(\mu \pm 1\sigma) \approx 68.27\%$, $P(\mu \pm 2\sigma) \approx 95.45\%$, $P(\mu \pm 3\sigma) \approx 99.73\%$.
Standard Normal Transformation: $\quad Z = \frac{X - \mu}{\sigma} \sim \mathcal{N}(0, 1)$

Central Limit Theorem (CLT)

If independent random variables $X_1, X_2, \dots, X_n$ are drawn from any population with mean $\mu$ and finite variance $\sigma^2$, then as $n \ge 30$, sample mean $\bar{X}$ approaches normality:

$$\begin{aligned} \bar{X} \sim \mathcal{N}\left(\mu, \frac{\sigma^2}{n}\right) \implies Z = \frac{\bar{X} - \mu}{\sigma / \sqrt{n}} \sim \mathcal{N}(0, 1) \end{aligned}$$

05. Priority 3: One-Way ANOVA & F-Test

Dedicated Deep Dive Available: Module 03
Contains all 5 ANOVA exam problems (May 2025 20M Iron Fe, Dec 2024 15M Technicians, Two-Way ANOVA), Family-Wise error proofs, and F-calculator.
Open Module 03 Guide →

Purpose: Tests equality of $k \ge 3$ independent group means simultaneously without inflating the Family-Wise Type I error rate ($\alpha_{\text{FW}} = 1 - (1-\alpha)^C$).

Hypotheses: $H_0: \mu_1 = \mu_2 = \dots = \mu_k$ vs $H_1: \text{At least two means differ}$.

The 6 Computational Steps:

  1. Correction Factor (CF): $\text{CF} = \frac{G^2}{N}$, where $G = \sum \sum x_{ij}$ (Grand Total) and $N = \sum n_j$.
  2. Total Sum of Squares (TSS): $\text{TSS} = \sum \sum X_{ij}^2 - \text{CF}$ ($df = N - 1$).
  3. Between Treatments SS (SSB): $\text{SSB} = \sum_{j=1}^k \frac{T_j^2}{n_j} - \text{CF}$ ($df = k - 1$).
  4. Within Groups Error SS (SSW): $\text{SSW} = \text{TSS} - \text{SSB}$ ($df = N - k$).
  5. Mean Squares: $\text{MSB} = \frac{\text{SSB}}{k - 1}$ and $\text{MSW} = \frac{\text{SSW}}{N - k}$.
  6. F-Ratio: $F_{\text{calc}} = \frac{\text{MSB}}{\text{MSW}}$ with degrees of freedom $\nu_1 = k - 1$ and $\nu_2 = N - k$.
Decision Rule:
If $F_{\text{calc}} > F_{\text{critical}}(k-1, N-k, \alpha)$, Reject $H_0$ and conclude there is a statistically significant difference between group means.

06. Priority 4: Chi-Square ($\chi^2$) Tests

Dedicated Deep Dive Available: Module 04
Contains all 5 Chi-Square exam problems (Dec 2024 10M Epidemic 2x2, Nov 2025 20M Poisson Fit & Pooling), Yates' formula, and interactive 2x2 solver.
Open Module 04 Guide →
$$\begin{aligned} \chi^2 = \sum \frac{(O - E)^2}{E} \sim \chi^2(df) \end{aligned}$$

1. Test of Independence (Contingency Table $r \times c$)

  • Expected frequency: $E_{ij} = \frac{(\text{Row } i \text{ Total}) \times (\text{Column } j \text{ Total})}{\text{Grand Total } N}$
  • Degrees of freedom: $df = (r - 1)(c - 1)$.
Yates' Correction for Continuity ($2 \times 2$ Contingency Table):
$$\begin{aligned} \chi_{\text{Yates}}^2 = \frac{N \left(|ad - bc| - \frac{N}{2}\right)^2}{(a + b)(c + d)(a + c)(b + d)}, \quad df = 1 \end{aligned}$$

2. Goodness-of-Fit Test

Degrees of freedom: $df = k - 1 - m$, where $k$ is number of classes after pooling small cells ($E_i \ge 5$), and $m$ is estimated parameters.

07. Priority 5: Tests of Means & Proportions (t-test & Z-test)

Dedicated Deep Dive Available: Module 05
Contains all 6 exam problems (Dec 2024 12M Quality Proportion Test & CI, Dec 2024 8M One-Sample t-Test, Paired t-tests, Pooled variance).
Open Module 05 Guide →

Core Test Formulas:

  • One-Sample Z-Test: $Z = \frac{\bar{x} - \mu_0}{\sigma / \sqrt{n}}$ (when $\sigma$ known or $n \ge 30$).
  • One-Sample t-Test: $t = \frac{\bar{x} - \mu_0}{s / \sqrt{n}}$ with $df = n - 1$.
  • Two-Sample Pooled t-Test: $t = \frac{\bar{x}_1 - \bar{x}_2}{s_p \sqrt{1/n_1 + 1/n_2}}$ where $s_p^2 = \frac{(n_1-1)s_1^2 + (n_2-1)s_2^2}{n_1+n_2-2}$ ($df = n_1 + n_2 - 2$).
  • Paired t-Test: $t = \frac{\bar{d}}{s_d / \sqrt{n}}$ with $df = n - 1$ where $d_i = x_{1i} - x_{2i}$.
  • One-Sample Proportion Z-Test: $Z = \frac{p - P_0}{\sqrt{\frac{P_0(1-P_0)}{n}}}$.
  • Two-Sample Proportion Z-Test: $Z = \frac{p_1 - p_2}{\sqrt{\bar{p}\bar{q}(1/n_1 + 1/n_2)}}$ where $\bar{p} = \frac{x_1 + x_2}{n_1 + n_2}$.

08. Descriptive Statistics, Moments, Skewness & Correlation

Measure Standard Formula Interpretation & Notes
Sample Variance ($s^2$) $s^2 = \frac{\sum (x_i - \bar{x})^2}{n - 1} = \frac{\sum x_i^2 - \frac{(\sum x_i)^2}{n}}{n - 1}$ Bessel's correction ($n-1$) ensures unbiased estimator.
Coefficient of Variation ($CV$) $CV = \frac{s}{\bar{x}} \times 100\%$ Relative dispersion; lower $CV$ indicates higher consistency.
Quartile Deviation ($QD$) $QD = \frac{Q_3 - Q_1}{2}, \quad \text{Coeff of } QD = \frac{Q_3 - Q_1}{Q_3 + Q_1}$ Semi-interquartile range; robust against extreme outliers.
Moment Conversions $\mu_2 = \mu_2' - (\mu_1')^2$
$\mu_3 = \mu_3' - 3\mu_2'\mu_1' + 2(\mu_1')^3$
$\mu_4 = \mu_4' - 4\mu_3'\mu_1' + 6\mu_2'(\mu_1')^2 - 3(\mu_1')^4$ 		Converts raw moments about origin ($\mu_r'$) to central moments ($\mu_r$).
Skewness ($\beta_1, \gamma_1$) $\beta_1 = \frac{\mu_3^2}{\mu_2^3}, \quad \gamma_1 = \frac{\mu_3}{\sigma^3}$ $\gamma_1 > 0$: Right/positive skew; $\gamma_1 < 0$: Left/negative skew.
Kurtosis ($\beta_2, \gamma_2$) $\beta_2 = \frac{\mu_4}{\mu_2^2}, \quad \gamma_2 = \beta_2 - 3$ $\beta_2 = 3$: Mesokurtic; $\beta_2 > 3$: Leptokurtic; $\beta_2 < 3$: Platykurtic.
Pearson Correlation ($r$) $r = \frac{\sum (x - \bar{x})(y - \bar{y})}{\sqrt{\sum (x - \bar{x})^2 \sum (y - \bar{y})^2}} = \frac{\operatorname{Cov}(X, Y)}{\sigma_X \sigma_Y}$ Bounded: $-1 \le r \le +1$. Measures linear association.
Spearman Rank Correlation ($r_s$) $r_s = 1 - \frac{6 \sum d_i^2}{n(n^2 - 1)}$ $d_i = R_{1i} - R_{2i}$. For tied ranks, add $\frac{m(m^2-1)}{12}$ to $\sum d^2$.

Power of the Test ($1 - \beta$):
The probability of correctly rejecting a false null hypothesis. Power increases when: (1) Sample size $n$ increases, (2) Effect size $|\mu - \mu_0|$ increases, or (3) Significance level $\alpha$ is relaxed.

10. Discrete Distributions (Binomial & Poisson)

Property Binomial Distribution: $B(n, p)$ Poisson Distribution: $P(\lambda)$
PMF $P(X = k)$ $\binom{n}{k} p^k (1-p)^{n-k}, \quad k \in \{0, 1, \dots, n\}$ $\frac{e^{-\lambda} \lambda^k}{k!}, \quad k \in \{0, 1, 2, \dots\}$
Mean ($\mathbb{E}[X]$) $\mu = np$ $\mu = \lambda$
Variance ($\operatorname{Var}(X)$) $\sigma^2 = np(1-p) = npq$ (always $< \text{Mean}$) $\sigma^2 = \lambda$ (Equidispersion: $\text{Mean} = \text{Variance}$)
Conditions for Applicability Fixed $n$ independent trials, binary outcomes, constant success probability $p$. Count of rare events occurring at a constant average rate $\lambda$ in continuous space/time.
Poisson as Limit of Binomial When $n \to \infty$ and $p \to 0$ such that $np = \lambda$ remains finite and constant.
