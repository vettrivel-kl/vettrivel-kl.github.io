Design of Experiments (DoE) & Taguchi Optimization

Master statistical experimental architecture. Linear mathematical models and complete ANOVA tables for Completely Randomized Design (CRD) vs. Randomized Block Design (RBD), field layouts with soil fertility blocks, and Taguchi $L_9(3^4)$ robust design with S/N ratios.

01. Intuition & Concept Contrast

Design of Experiments (DoE) manipulates input variables systematically to identify causal mechanisms while minimizing the confounding impact of environmental noise.

Unit Homogeneity

CRD vs RBD Experimental Units

Completely Randomized Design (CRD): Assumes experimental units are strictly homogeneous (e.g., identical test tubes in a laboratory). Assigns treatments purely at random across all units.

Randomized Block Design (RBD): Used when units are heterogeneous across an identifiable nuisance gradient (e.g. soil fertility, operator skill). Groups units into homogeneous blocks to isolate and subtract nuisance variance.

Matrix Efficiency

Full Factorial vs Taguchi Orthogonal Array

Full Factorial ($3^3 = 27$ runs): Tests every possible combination of 3 factors at 3 levels. Expensive and time-consuming in physical manufacturing.

Taguchi Orthogonal Array ($L_9$): Balances levels pairwise across columns. Tests all main factor effects in just **9 runs** ($67\%$ reduction in experimental cost).

02. Linear Models & ANOVA Partitioning (CRD vs RBD)

Completely Randomized Design (One-Way ANOVA)
$$Y_{ij} = \mu + \tau_i + \epsilon_{ij}, \qquad \epsilon_{ij} \sim \text{i.i.d. } \mathcal{N}(0, \sigma^2)$$ $Y_{ij}$: Observation of $j$-th replicate under $i$-th treatment. $\tau_i$: Treatment effect ($\sum \tau_i = 0$).
Randomized Block Design (Two-Way ANOVA Without Replication)
$$Y_{ij} = \mu + \tau_i + \beta_j + \epsilon_{ij}, \qquad \epsilon_{ij} \sim \text{i.i.d. } \mathcal{N}(0, \sigma^2)$$ $\beta_j$: True block effect ($\sum \beta_j = 0$). Residual error $df = (k - 1)(b - 1)$.

03. Taguchi Robust Parameter Design & S/N Analysis

Nov 2025 (Q6.ii) Guaranteed 10-Mark Question. Optimizing 3 factors at 3 levels.

1. Selection of the $L_9(3^4)$ Orthogonal Array:

Full factorial testing requires $3^3 = \mathbf{27 \text{ runs}}$.

Minimum required degrees of freedom:

$$\text{DoF} = 1 + \sum_{i=1}^3 (\text{Levels}_i - 1) = 1 + 3 \times (3 - 1) = 1 + 6 = \mathbf{7}$$

The standard **Taguchi $L_9(3^4)$ Orthogonal Array** provides 8 degrees of freedom in only **9 runs**, perfectly accommodating 3 factors at 3 levels!

The 3 Taguchi Signal-to-Noise (S/N) Ratio Formulas
$$\begin{aligned} \text{Larger-the-Better (Efficiency, Yield):} & \quad \eta = -10 \log_{10}\left( \frac{1}{n} \sum_{i=1}^n \frac{1}{y_i^2} \right) \\[6pt] \text{Smaller-the-Better (Defects, Errors):} & \quad \eta = -10 \log_{10}\left( \frac{1}{n} \sum_{i=1}^n y_i^2 \right) \\[6pt] \text{Nominal-the-Best (Precision Dimensions):} & \quad \eta = 10 \log_{10}\left( \frac{\bar{y}^2}{s^2} \right) \end{aligned}$$ Rule: Irrespective of objective type, HIGHER S/N ratio always indicates optimal performance.

05. Repeatable Step-by-Step Solving Protocol

Step 1: Identify Nuisance Factor & Select Design

If experimental units have a known gradient (e.g. soil fertility, machines), choose **Randomized Block Design (RBD)**.

Step 2: Draw Physical Layout Matrix

Draw $b$ blocks. Inside each block, assign all $k$ treatments at random: $N = k \times b$.

Step 3: State the Mathematical Linear Model

Write $Y_{ij} = \mu + \tau_i + \beta_j + \epsilon_{ij}$ with assumptions $\epsilon_{ij} \sim \mathcal{N}(0, \sigma^2)$ and $\sum \tau_i = \sum \beta_j = 0$.

Step 4: Compute ANOVA Degrees of Freedom $$df_{\text{Total}} = kb - 1, \quad df_{\text{Treat}} = k - 1, \quad df_{\text{Blocks}} = b - 1, \quad df_{\text{Error}} = (k-1)(b-1)$$
Step 5: For Taguchi Optimization, Select Orthogonal Array

Compute minimum $\text{DoF} = 1 + \sum (L_i - 1)$. Recommend $L_9(3^4)$ and state the appropriate S/N formula.

06. 6 Deadly Exam Traps & Common Student Mistakes

Trap 01: Error Degrees of Freedom Confusion

Writing $df_{\text{Error}} = N - k$ in RBD

In CRD, error $df$ is $N - k$. In RBD, **block degrees of freedom ($b - 1$) are subtracted**, leaving $\mathbf{(k - 1)(b - 1)}$. Writing $N - k$ in RBD loses 4 marks.

Trap 02: Inverting S/N Ratio Selection

Picking Lower S/N for "Smaller-the-Better"

Students assume that for defects (smaller-the-better), they should look for a lower S/N ratio. FALSE! The negative sign ($-10 \log_{10}$) in the formula inverts it. **HIGHER S/N ratio is ALWAYS better.**

Trap 03: Recommending CRD for Field Studies

Ignoring Soil Fertility Differences

Soil fertility is never uniform across fields. Recommending CRD allows fertile plots to confound fertilizer results. RBD is strictly mandatory.

Trap 04: Full Factorial Run Exaggeration

Running 27 Runs Instead of 9

In the Taguchi question, failing to recommend the $L_9$ array and writing code for 27 full-factorial runs misses the entire purpose of Taguchi optimization.

Trap 05: Omitting Linear Model Assumptions

Writing Just the ANOVA Table

Examiners require the linear model equations ($Y_{ij} = \mu + \dots$) and error assumptions ($\epsilon_{ij} \sim \text{i.i.d. } \mathcal{N}(0, \sigma^2)$).

Trap 06: Missing Interaction Caveat in Taguchi

Assuming L9 Resolves Heavy Interactions

Standard $L_9$ orthogonal arrays assume factor interactions are negligible. If interactions are severe, full factorial or fractional factorial resolution IV is required.

07. Complete University Question Bank (All Past PYQs Solved)

Nov 2025 · Question 6 Fertilizer Field Layout in RBD & Taguchi L9 Optimization (20 Marks)
Question Statement:
i) A researcher studies the effect of 3 fertilizers (A, B, C) on crop yield across 4 fields with differing soil fertility levels. Design an appropriate experimental layout and explain suitability. (10 Marks)
ii) Optimize 3 factors (A, B, C) each at 3 levels: explain Taguchi method, suggest suitable orthogonal array, and describe S/N ratio calculation/interpretation. (10 Marks)

Solution:

(i) Fertilizer Field Layout via Randomized Block Design (RBD) (10 Marks):

1. Design Selection: **Randomized Block Design (RBD)** with $k = 3$ treatments (Fertilizers A, B, C) and $b = 4$ blocks (Fields 1 to 4).

2. Justification: Soil fertility is a known nuisance variable. If CRD were used, Fertilizer A could land on the most fertile field by chance. RBD groups plots into 4 homogeneous blocks; within each block, all 3 fertilizers are assigned at random. Field-to-field variance is explicitly removed from residual error.

3. Experimental Layout: Total plots $N = 3 \times 4 = \mathbf{12}$.

4. ANOVA Partitioning & Degrees of Freedom:

  • $df_{\text{Fertilizer}} = k - 1 = 3 - 1 = \mathbf{2}$
  • $df_{\text{Fields (Blocks)}} = b - 1 = 4 - 1 = \mathbf{3}$
  • $df_{\text{Error}} = (k - 1)(b - 1) = 2 \times 3 = \mathbf{6}$
  • $df_{\text{Total}} = kb - 1 = 12 - 1 = \mathbf{11}$
(ii) Taguchi Parameter Optimization & S/N Ratios (10 Marks):

1. Taguchi Concept: Maximizes robustness against noise without expensive component tolerance costs.

2. Suitable Orthogonal Array: 3 factors at 3 levels requires 27 runs in full factorial. Minimum degrees of freedom is $1 + 3 \times 2 = 7$. The **Taguchi $L_9(3^4)$ Orthogonal Array** executes this in just **9 runs**.

3. S/N Ratios:

  • Larger-the-Better (Yield): $\eta = -10 \log_{10}\left(\frac{1}{n}\sum \frac{1}{y^2}\right)$
  • Smaller-the-Better (Defects): $\eta = -10 \log_{10}\left(\frac{1}{n}\sum y^2\right)$
  • Nominal-the-Best: $\eta = 10 \log_{10}\left(\frac{\bar{y}^2}{s^2}\right)$

Interpretation: A higher S/N ratio always corresponds to lower variability and superior quality.

Jan 2025 · Question 4 CRD vs RBD Linear Models, ANOVA Tables & Industrial Examples (20 Marks)
Question Statement:
Explain completely randomized design and randomized block design with suitable examples. (20 Marks)

Solution:

1. Completely Randomized Design (CRD) (10 Marks):
  • Prerequisite: Homogeneous experimental units (e.g. uniform laboratory test tubes).
  • Linear Model: $Y_{ij} = \mu + \tau_i + \epsilon_{ij}$, where $\epsilon_{ij} \sim \mathcal{N}(0, \sigma^2)$.
  • Example: Testing 3 neural network optimizers (SGD, Adam, RMSprop) across 15 runs on identical cloud GPUs ($k = 3, N = 15$).
  • ANOVA Table: Treatments $df = 2$, Error $df = 12$, Total $df = 14$.
2. Randomized Block Design (RBD) (10 Marks):
  • Prerequisite: Heterogeneous units across a known gradient. Units grouped into homogeneous blocks.
  • Linear Model: $Y_{ij} = \mu + \tau_i + \beta_j + \epsilon_{ij}$, where $\beta_j$ is block effect and $\epsilon_{ij} \sim \mathcal{N}(0, \sigma^2)$.
  • Example: Testing 4 lathe tool coatings across 3 machinists with varying skill levels ($k = 4, b = 3, N = 12$).
  • ANOVA Table: Treatments $df = 3$, Blocks $df = 2$, Error $df = (4-1)(3-1) = 6$, Total $df = 11$.
