01. Intuition & Concept Contrast

Statistical inference separates genuine experimental signals from random sampling fluctuations.

Analysis Scale

Correlation vs Chi-Square Test

Pearson Correlation ($r$): Evaluates the strength and direction of a linear relationship between two continuous numerical variables ($X, Y \in \mathbb{R}$). $r \in [-1, +1]$.

Chi-Square Goodness-of-Fit ($\chi^2$): Tests whether observed frequency counts across discrete categorical bins conform to a theoretical distribution (e.g. uniform distribution).

Causality Caution

Association vs Causation

Correlation ($r = 0.99$): Measures co-movement. Does NOT prove that $X$ causes $Y$ (could be driven by a lurking confounding variable $Z$).

ANOVA Experimentation ($F = 57.15$): When treatments are assigned randomly under experimental control, significant differences prove direct causality.

02. Karl Pearson's Correlation Coefficient ($r$)

Jan 2025 (Q5.i) Guaranteed 10-Mark Numerical. Father ($X$) and Son ($Y$) heights.

The Product-Moment Correlation Formula
$$r = \frac{\sum_{i=1}^n (X_i - \bar{X})(Y_i - \bar{Y})}{\sqrt{\sum_{i=1}^n (X_i - \bar{X})^2 \sum_{i=1}^n (Y_i - \bar{Y})^2}}$$ Where $\bar{X} = \frac{1}{n}\sum X_i$ and $\bar{Y} = \frac{1}{n}\sum Y_i$.

Step-by-Step Verified Arithmetic ($n = 8$ pairs):

1. Means: $\sum X = 544 \implies \bar{X} = 68.0 \text{ inches}$. $\sum Y = 552 \implies \bar{Y} = 69.0 \text{ inches}$.

2. Sum of squared deviations:

$$\sum (X_i - \bar{X})^2 = (-3)^2 + (-2)^2 + (-1)^2 + (-1)^2 + 0^2 + 1^2 + 2^2 + 4^2 = 9+4+1+1+0+1+4+16 = \mathbf{36}$$ $$\sum (Y_i - \bar{Y})^2 = (-2)^2 + (-1)^2 + (-4)^2 + (-1)^2 + 3^2 + 3^2 + 0^2 + 2^2 = 4+1+16+1+9+9+0+4 = \mathbf{44}$$

3. Sum of cross products:

$$\sum (X_i - \bar{X})(Y_i - \bar{Y}) = 6 + 2 + 4 + 1 + 0 + 3 + 0 + 8 = \mathbf{24}$$

4. Exact algebraic resolution:

$$r = \frac{24}{\sqrt{36 \times 44}} = \frac{24}{6 \sqrt{44}} = \frac{4}{2\sqrt{11}} = \mathbf{\frac{2}{\sqrt{11}} \approx +0.6030}$$

Interpretation: A moderate-to-strong positive linear correlation ($r = +0.6030$). Taller fathers tend to have taller sons.

03. Chi-Square Goodness-of-Fit Test ($\chi^2$)

Jan 2025 (Q5.ii) Guaranteed 10-Mark Problem. Uniform distribution of aircraft accidents.

The Chi-Square Test Statistic
$$\chi^2 = \sum_{i=1}^k \frac{(O_i - E_i)^2}{E_i}, \qquad df = k - 1$$ $O_i$: Observed frequency. $E_i$: Expected frequency under $H_0$.

Step-by-Step Verified Resolution ($k = 6$ days):

1. Total Accidents: $N = 14 + 18 + 12 + 11 + 15 + 14 = \mathbf{84}$.

2. Expected frequency under null hypothesis $H_0$ (uniform distribution):

$$E_i = \frac{N}{k} = \frac{84}{6} = \mathbf{14.0 \text{ accidents per day}}$$

3. Deviations squared $(O_i - E_i)^2$:

  • Mon: $(14 - 14)^2 = 0$
  • Tue: $(18 - 14)^2 = 16$
  • Wed: $(12 - 14)^2 = 4$
  • Thu: $(11 - 14)^2 = 9$
  • Fri: $(15 - 14)^2 = 1$
  • Sat: $(14 - 14)^2 = 0$
  • Sum of squared deviations $= 0 + 16 + 4 + 9 + 1 + 0 = \mathbf{30}$

4. Test statistic calculation:

$$\chi^2_{\text{calc}} = \frac{30}{14} = \frac{15}{7} \approx \mathbf{2.1429}$$

5. Critical value comparison: $df = 6 - 1 = 5$. At $\alpha = 0.05$, critical table value $\chi^2_{0.05, 5} = \mathbf{11.070}$.

$$\mathbf{\chi^2_{\text{calc}} = 2.1429 < \chi^2_{\text{table}} = 11.070 \implies \text{Fail to Reject } H_0}$$

Conclusion: Empirical data confirms accidents are uniformly distributed over the week ($p \approx 0.829$).

06. Repeatable Step-by-Step Solving Protocol

Step 1: Compute Means $\bar{X}$ and $\bar{Y}$

Ensure means are exact. In the exam problem, $\bar{X} = 68.0$ and $\bar{Y} = 69.0$ are exact integers.

Step 2: Construct Direct Deviation Columns

Create columns $(X - \bar{X})$, $(Y - \bar{Y})$, squared deviations, and cross products. Verify sums of deviations equal zero.

Step 3: Simplify Fraction to Surd Form $$r = \frac{24}{\sqrt{36 \times 44}} = \frac{4}{2\sqrt{11}} = \frac{2}{\sqrt{11}} \approx \mathbf{+0.6030}$$
Step 4: For Chi-Square, State Hypotheses Explicitly

Write $H_0$: Accidents are uniformly distributed; $H_1$: Accidents are not uniformly distributed.

Step 5: Compute $\chi^2$ and Compare with Critical Table Value

Show $\chi^2_{\text{calc}} = 2.14 < \chi^2_{0.05, 5} = 11.070 \implies$ **Fail to reject $H_0$**.

07. 6 Deadly Exam Traps & Common Student Mistakes

Trap 01: Degrees of Freedom in Chi-Square

Using $df = k$ Instead of $k - 1$

Degrees of freedom for a goodness-of-fit test is **$k - 1$**. For 6 days, $df = 6 - 1 = \mathbf{5}$. Using $df = 6$ uses the wrong critical value.

Trap 02: Denominator in Chi-Square

Dividing by $O_i$ Instead of $E_i$

The denominator must be the expected frequency $E_i$ ($\frac{(O-E)^2}{E}$). Dividing by $O_i$ invalidates the statistic.

Trap 03: Inverting Hypothesis Decision Rule

Rejecting $H_0$ When $\chi^2_{\text{calc}} < \chi^2_{\text{table}}$

We reject $H_0$ only when calculated value **EXCEEDS** table value ($\chi^2_{\text{calc}} > \chi^2_{\text{table}}$). Since $2.14 < 11.07$, we **fail to reject $H_0$**.

Trap 04: Correlation Denominator Radical

Forgetting Square Root in $r$ Denominator

The denominator is $\sqrt{\sum dx^2 \sum dy^2}$. Writing $\sum dx^2 \sum dy^2$ yields an absurd number like $0.015$.

Trap 05: Sunday Omission in Accident Data

Assuming 7 Days When Table Gives 6

Read the table carefully! The exam table lists **6 days (Mon to Sat)**, with total 84 accidents. Dividing by 7 gives $E=12$, which is incorrect. Divide by 6 to get $E = 14.0$.

Trap 06: Stating Correlation Proves Causation

Claiming Exercise Directly Causes Diet Weight Loss

Correlation indicates linear association, not mechanistic causality. Only controlled experimental designs prove causality.
