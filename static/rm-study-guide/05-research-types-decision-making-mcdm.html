01. Intuition & Concept Contrast

Research methodology provides the formal compass for systematic discovery, while decision-making frameworks ensure empirical choices are transparent and reproducible.

Inquiry Purpose

Exploratory vs Causal Research

Exploratory Research: Conducted when the problem is poorly understood or novel. Flexible and qualitative; formulates questions and working hypotheses.

Causal (Hypothesis-Testing): Conducted under controlled experimental conditions to prove whether variable $X$ directly causes changes in response $Y$.

Cognitive Balance

Critical Thinking vs Creative Thinking

Critical Thinking (Auditing): Rigorous, analytical verification. Audits sensor calibration, controls for confounding variables, checks statistical power.

Creative Thinking (Reframing): Lateral, generative hypothesis formulation. Envisions novel mechanisms, non-linear interactions, and new conceptual models.

04. Balancing Critical vs. Creative Thinking in Research Anomaly

Nov 2025 (Q2.i) 10-Mark Question. Resolving empirical divergence from established literature.

Auditing & Rigor

Critical Thinking Protocol

  • Audit Instrument Validity: Check questionnaire reliability (Cronbach's $\alpha \ge 0.70$) and sensor calibration drift.
  • Check Sampling Bias: Confirm sample is representative and not skewed by volunteer selection effects.
  • Examine Confounders: Identify unmeasured moderators (socioeconomic status, device access, digital literacy).
Hypothesis Reframing

Creative Thinking Protocol

  • Formulate Novel Hypotheses: Could post-pandemic screen fatigue or cognitive overload explain the divergence?
  • Explore Non-Linear Models: Look for U-shaped or threshold interaction dynamics rather than simple linear trends.
  • Mixed-Methods Expansion: Conduct follow-up qualitative focus group interviews to capture unrecorded nuances.

05. Intellectual Property Rights (IPR) for Engineering Inventions

Nov 2025 (Q2.ii) 10-Mark Question. Strategic value of protecting green eco-friendly products.

Strategic Advantages for Student Innovators:

  1. 20-Year Legal Market Monopoly: A utility patent grants exclusive rights to make, use, and sell the invention, preventing corporate competitors from copying the green product.
  2. Venture Capital & Investor Leverage: Institutional VC funds and angel investors rarely invest in unpatented hardware due to risk of instant reverse-engineering.
  3. Commercial Licensing & Royalties: Enables licensing the technology to manufacturing multinationals for recurring revenue without owning factories.
  4. Academic & Career Prestige: Granted patents dramatically accelerate postgraduate fellowship applications, PhD admissions, and international mobility.

07. 6 Deadly Exam Traps & Common Student Mistakes

Trap 01: Unweighted MCDM Tables

Treating All Criteria as Equal

In MCDM, processor performance cannot have the same weight as warranty. Always assign explicit normalized weights ($\sum w_i = 1.0$) to show analytical maturity.

Trap 02: Missing Flowchart in Decision Making

Writing Only Paragraphs for Question 1.c

Question 1.c explicitly asks to "Draw a flowchart showing your decision-making steps". Omitting the flowchart loses 4 marks automatically.

Trap 03: Confusing Pure with Applied Research

Claiming Pure Research Solves Immediate Problems

Pure (Fundamental) research expands theoretical laws of nature without immediate commercial application. Applied research solves specific practical problems.

Trap 04: One-Sided Response in Empirical Divergence

Focusing Only on Critical or Only on Creative Thinking

The prompt asks to balance both. You must show the auditing phase (Critical) and the reframing phase (Creative).

Trap 05: Exceeding Budget Constraint

Recommending Phones Above ₹24,999

Recommending a ₹26,000 phone fails the primary budget constraint. All compared phones must be strictly $\le \text{₹24,999}$.

Trap 06: Treating Patents as Eternal

Claiming Patents Last Forever

Utility patents expire after **20 years** from filing date, after which the invention enters the public domain. Trademarks can be renewed perpetually.

1. Objectives of Research (6 Marks):

Discovery of new facts, verification of existing laws, development of theoretical models, and solving real-world industrial problems.

  • Exploratory vs Causal: Exploratory explores vague domains; Causal proves cause-and-effect relationships.
  • Pure vs Applied: Pure advances fundamental scientific knowledge; Applied solves immediate commercial challenges.

Correct: (b) In weighted linear utility models, attribute weights are normalized so their sum strictly equals $1.0$.
2. How long does a standard utility patent grant exclusive commercial rights to an inventor from filing date?
Correct: (c) Under international patent law (TRIPS), utility patents grant exclusive rights for **20 years** from the date of filing.
3. When research results diverge from established scientific consensus, what is the role of Critical Thinking?
Correct: (a) Critical thinking acts as an investigative audit, ensuring the anomaly is not caused by experimental error or sampling bias.

Correct: (c) Cross-sectional takes an instantaneous snapshot; longitudinal tracks temporal trajectories over months or years.
